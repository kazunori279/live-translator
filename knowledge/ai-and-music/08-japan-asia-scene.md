# Japan and Asia

**TL;DR for the assistant:** Japan has the most permissive AI-training copyright rule in the G7 — Article 30-4 of the Copyright Act, which the Waseda professor who coined the phrase called a "Machine-Learning Paradise" back in 2017 — but the Agency for Cultural Affairs narrowed it in March 2024, and JASRAC is now openly campaigning to amend it, saying 54% of the music creators it surveyed in 2026 object to their work being used for training. Japan also has the world's deepest precedent for loving a synthetic voice: Hatsune Miku turns 20 on 31 August 2027, her live series has drawn over 580,000 people since 2013, and a brand-new Miku V6 shipped on 14 April 2026 — so "the public will never accept a synthetic singer" is not an argument you can make in Tokyo. The rest of Asia is diverging fast: China made AI-content labelling legally mandatory on 1 September 2025, Korea's AI Framework Act took effect on 22 January 2026, and Japan's own AI Promotion Act, fully in force since 1 September 2025, deliberately has no penalties at all. And the cautionary tale is right next door: HYBE resolved on 15 July 2026 to liquidate Supertone, the AI-voice company behind MIDNATT, after sinking about 35 million dollars into it.

## Key facts

### Japan: the copyright rule everyone argues about

- **Article 30-4 of Japan's Copyright Act** permits exploiting a work "in any way and to the extent necessary" where it is *not* the purpose to personally enjoy, or cause another to enjoy, the thoughts or sentiments expressed in the work. "Information analysis" (TDM) is the named example. It applies to **commercial** actors, has **no opt-out for rights holders**, **no lawful-access requirement**, and no limit on who does the analysis — which is why it is genuinely broader than the EU, UK or Singapore TDM exceptions ([Ueno, WIPO-hosted paper on the General Understanding, 2024/2025](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf)).
- **The lineage matters for accuracy**: Japan introduced the world's first broad TDM exception in the **2009** amendment (then Art. 47-7); the current Art. 30-4 came in with the **2018** amendment. Getting this wrong in Tokyo is the kind of thing lawyers in the room will notice ([Ueno / WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf)).
- **"Machine-Learning Paradise" is a real, attributable coinage**, not journalist shorthand: Tatsuhiro Ueno (Professor, Waseda University; director of RCLIP) used 機械学習パラダイス in an RCLIP column dated **9 September 2017** and again in the AI journal *Jinkōchinō* 36-6 (2021), p.745. Ueno himself insists it covers **only the training phase** and "does not in any way allow copyright infringement in the output phase" ([Ueno / WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf)).
- **15 March 2024** — the Legal Subcommittee of the Copyright Subdivision of the Cultural Council published **"AIと著作権に関する考え方について" (General Understanding on AI and Copyright)** after seven sessions from 26 July 2023 to 29 February 2024. It is **explicitly not legally binding** and is not a legal assessment of any specific product ([Agency for Cultural Affairs, English overview, May 2024, PDF](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf); [ACA portal page](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/aiandcopyright.html)).
- **How the 2024 guidance narrowed 30-4** — three concrete carve-outs a panel can name:
  1. **Coexisting "enjoyment" purpose kills the exception.** If additional/incremental training on an existing model is done intentionally so the model will output the original expression of the training works, Art. 30-4 does not apply. Even *unintentional* cases using "a small amount of training data" for incremental learning can fall into this bucket.
  2. **The proviso** ("unreasonably prejudice the interests of the copyright owner") bites where you scrape a site instead of paying for its **TDM-ready paid API**, or where you circumvent **technical measures** blocking AI crawlers on a site that has a track record of licensing data.
  3. **Dependence (依拠性) is generally presumed at the output stage** if the AI learned from the work — even if the user had no idea. ([Ueno / WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf))
- **The one place the committee openly split**: whether training a model *exhaustively on one creator* (the "Studio Ghibli / Akira Toriyama / Beatles model" hypothetical) triggers the proviso when the outputs share only style and worldview, not protected expression. The prevailing view says no. A minority — recorded in the **main text rather than a footnote**, which is unusual — said displacement of demand for that creator could qualify. That is the live fault line ([Ueno / WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf)).
- **31 July 2024** — ACA published an **AI and Copyright Checklist & Guidance** for developers, rights holders and users ([ACA](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/aiandcopyright.html)).
- **AI Promotion Act (人工知能関連技術の研究開発及び活用の推進に関する法律)**, Act No. 53 of 2025, promulgated **4 June 2025**, **fully in force 1 September 2025**. It creates an **AI Strategy Headquarters** in the Cabinet and an AI Basic Plan. It is a *promotion* law, not the EU AI Act: **no penalties**, only investigation, guidance, and reputational exposure ([Cabinet Office CSTP](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_act/ai_act.html)).

### Japan: the industry position

- **24 July 2023** — JASRAC's board-resolved statement on generative AI. Four points, and the language is sharp: allowing commercial AI development to use works freely is **フリーライド (a free ride)** and "is not fair." JASRAC also flags that Japan is **the only G7 country** with a copyright limitation built on the absence of an "enjoyment" purpose ([JASRAC, 24 Jul 2023](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/release/23/07_3.html)).
- **27 June 2025** — JASRAC's creator survey (fielded 20 Jan – 28 Feb 2025, **n = 970** analysed from 1,325 respondents): on developers using their works as AI training data, **53% opposed or somewhat opposed, 23% supported or somewhat supported** ([JASRAC](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/information/release/25/250627.html); [Musicman, 30 Jun 2025](https://www.musicman.co.jp/business/681869)).
- **11 June 2026** — JASRAC launched a dedicated campaign page, *"創造のサイクルとの調和がとれたAI利活用の実現に向けて"*, explicitly calling for **amendment of Article 30-4**. Its ask is framed as **"securing the opportunity to choose"** — an opt-out/consent mechanism — **not a ban on AI** ([JASRAC](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/aboutus/ai.html); [JASRAC activity timeline](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/information/26/ai-effort.html)). Its 2026 survey (Jan–Mar 2026) is reported at **54% opposed vs 20% supportive** ([Innovatopia, Jun 2026](https://innovatopia.jp/tech-social/tech-social-news/108475/)).
- **17 December 2025** — the **AIに関する音楽団体協議会** (Music Industry Council on AI), now **nine bodies** — JASRAC, RIAJ, NexTone, CPRA, FMPJ, MPA (Japan), JAME, FCA and ACPC — restated its demands: a **legal mandate on AI developers to record, retain and disclose training data**, and **legal liability on the developer/provider** for infringing outputs rather than leaving takedowns to individual creators. The statement **names Suno AI and Sora 2** as services whose spread has created an "extremely serious" situation ([JASRAC](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/information/release/25/251217.html); [Musicman, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.musicman.co.jp/business/705807)).
- **Both Japanese CMOs apply the same registration test**: a work is administrable only if there is **human creative contribution**. Output generated autonomously from a simple prompt is not registered — by **JASRAC** ([JASRAC AI page](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/aboutus/ai.html)) and by **NexTone**, which allows registration where the human author's creativity is present even if AI was used in part ([NexTone rights-management service](https://www.nex-tone.co.jp/copyright/users/)).
- **28 October 2025** — **CODA**, the Japanese content trade body (members include Aniplex, Bandai Namco, Studio Ghibli, Square Enix, Kadokawa, Shueisha and RIAJ), formally asked OpenAI to stop training on member content for Sora 2. Its core argument is the one that generalises to music: **Japanese copyright law requires prior permission, and there is no mechanism by which a later objection ("opt-out") avoids liability** ([Automaton West, Oct 2025](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/sonys-aniplex-bandai-namco-and-other-japanese-publishers-demand-end-to-unauthorized-training-of-openais-sora2-through-coda/)).

### Japan: the synthetic-voice precedent nobody else has

- **Hatsune Miku shipped 31 August 2007** from Crypton Future Media (Sapporo), voiced by actress Saki Fujita. She turns **20 on 31 August 2027**; the anniversary project launched **9 March 2026**, and — very much in character — the 20th-anniversary costume was **crowdsourced from fans on piapro**, with entries closing 17 April 2026 ([Crypton](https://www.crypton.co.jp/cfm/news/2026/03/09miku20th)).
- **Magical Mirai**, the Miku concert-plus-exhibition series, drew **over 85,000 people across three cities in 2025** (Sendai 1–3 Aug, Osaka 9–11 Aug, Tokyo 29–31 Aug) and **over 580,000 cumulatively since 2013** ([Crypton, 5 Sep 2025](https://www.crypton.co.jp/cfm/news/2025/09/05mm25); [Crypton, Mar 2026](https://www.crypton.co.jp/cfm/news/2026/03/09miku20th)). 2026 adds **Hamamatsu** — Yamaha's home town, where VOCALOID was invented.
- **The licensing model is the real lesson.** The **piapro Character License (PCL, 2009)** — mirrored as **CC BY-NC 3.0** for non-Japanese users — lets anyone use Miku, Kagamine Rin/Len, Megurine Luka, MEIKO and KAITO **non-commercially without asking**; commercial use routes through piapro Link. Crypton later opened **YouTube monetisation to amateur creators**. Japan built a 20-year fandom on a *pre-cleared* derivative-works licence, which is exactly the thing generative-AI platforms are being sued for not having ([piapro, English](https://piapro.net/intl/en_for_creators.html)).
- **Crypton is not uncritically pro-AI, though.** From **28 November 2023**, works posted to piapro that are wholly or mostly AI-generated must be filed under an **"AI生成" category and can no longer carry any licence terms** — retroactively stripped from previously posted AI works. In other words, the platform that invented open remix licensing **withheld** that licence from AI output ([piapro ch, Nov 2023](https://ch.piapro.jp/creator/2023/11/fr2311102)).
- **Miku V6 shipped 14 April 2026** on Yamaha's VOCALOID6 engine using **VOCALOID:AI** — automatic breath insertion, automatic English/Japanese routing, "Original" and "Soft" voicebanks, Chinese to follow. ¥24,200 for the starter pack, ¥10,780 voicebank-only ([Crypton, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.crypton.co.jp/cfm/news/2026/02/18miku_v6)).
- **Synthesizer V** (Dreamtonics, founded by **Kanru Hua**, Tokyo) is the professional standard: a hybrid of concatenative and neural synthesis, **Studio 2 released 21 March 2025**, supporting English, Japanese, Cantonese, Mandarin, Spanish and Korean ([Wikipedia: Synthesizer V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer_V)).
- **NEUTRINO** (by "SHACHI", free, first released February 2020, famous as "AI Kiritan") is the grassroots counterweight — and its pitch is explicitly ethical: **"Clean data & clean model,"** built only on voice data recorded with the voice actor's consent or from public research institutions. As of May 2026 it offers **17 voice libraries** and has reached a third-generation ("Tau") engine after a diffusion-based second generation ("Muon") ([NEUTRINO official](https://studio-neutrino.com/)).
- **CeVIO AI** (CeVIO Project, from Nagoya Institute of Technology speech-synthesis lineage; Song Editor **January 2021**) is the third pillar, notable because its voice catalogue is co-produced with labels and studios — including **KAMITSUBAKI STUDIO**, home of virtual singer Kaf — i.e. living artists deliberately shipping their own voice as a licensed instrument ([CeVIO AI products](https://cevio.jp/products_cevio_ai/)).
- **SOUNDRAW** (Tokyo) is Japan's best-known generative-music startup, and it competes on provenance rather than raw capability: **"SOUNDRAW's AI is trained only on music we create in-house,"** 100% self-produced catalogue, perpetual worldwide commercial licence, partners listed including SoftBank, Canva and LG ([soundraw.io](https://soundraw.io/)).

### Korea

- **MIDNATT** — alter ego of BIGHIT MUSIC artist **Lee Hyun** — released **"Masquerade" on 15 May 2023 in six languages** (Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Spanish, Vietnamese) using Supertone's voice tech, including native-speaker pronunciation data and a synthesised female vocal layer over his own voice ([MBW, May 2023](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/hybe-just-released-a-k-pop-track-sung-in-six-different-languages-using-ai-voice-technology/)).
- **And then it ended.** Supertone's shareholders resolved on **15 July 2026** to dissolve and liquidate the company; HYBE disclosed it on **23 July 2026**. HYBE had invested roughly **KRW 49bn (~US$35m)** in total (KRW 4bn for 18.2% in 2021, then ~KRW 45bn / ~US$32m for control). Supertone's **2025 revenue was KRW 2.3bn (~US$1.6m) against a KRW 15.4bn (~US$10.5m) operating loss**; intra-group revenue from HYBE fell from KRW 200m in 2023 to KRW 61m. HYBE is trying to sell the Shift/Clear/Air tools ([MBW, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/hybe-winds-down-ai-voice-company-supertone-after-investing-nearly-35m/)).
- **Fully AI K-pop already exists and has fans.** **GLXE**, a three-member all-AI male "group" whose voices and songs are made with **Suno**, debuted in **December 2025** with a fandom name ("Stars"), dance challenges and listening parties. Earlier AI-adjacent acts: **aespa** (2020, human members plus designed avatars), **MAVE:**, and Supertone's short-lived **SYNDI8** (2024) ([Dazed, 2026](https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/70013/1/k-pop-has-a-generative-ai-problem-glxe-suno)).
- **The backlash is mostly about consent, not craft.** Non-consensual AI "self-insert" content of idols, real-time face-swap filters demoed in **March 2026** using female idols, and agency AI chatbots of artists (RBW) have all drawn organised fan campaigns ([Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/70013/1/k-pop-has-a-generative-ai-problem-glxe-suno)).
- **Korea's AI Framework Act / AI Basic Act** (Act No. 20676, promulgated **21 January 2025**) took effect **22 January 2026**, with its Enforcement Decree the same day — all provisions at once, unlike the EU's phased rollout. Generative-AI providers must **notify users up front and label outputs** that are hard to distinguish from human-made content. **10²⁶ FLOPs** of cumulative training compute triggers "high-performance AI" duties. It applies **extraterritorially**. Fines top out at **KRW 30m (~US$21,000)**, and MSIT has signalled a **one-year grace period** on fines through 2026 ([Cooley, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); [CSET English translation](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/south-korea-ai-law-2025/)).

### China

- **AI-content labelling is legally mandatory.** The **Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content**, issued **14 March 2025** by CAC, MIIT, the Ministry of Public Security and NRTA, took effect **1 September 2025**, alongside a **mandatory national standard**. Two obligations: a **visible explicit label** on content likely to confuse, and an **implicit label embedded in metadata of all AI-generated content**. Distribution platforms must check metadata and add their own identifiers; removing or tampering with labels — or supplying tools to do so — is prohibited ([Loeb & Loeb, Mar 2025](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1)).
- **Tencent Music (TME)**, Q1 2026 reported **12 May 2026**: total revenue **RMB 7.90bn (+7.3% YoY)**, music services **RMB 6.51bn (+12.2%)**, membership **RMB 4.57bn (+6.6%)**. Management's framing: AI is "broadening participation in content creation" and **AI-generated songs account for a growing share of daily new releases** — but TME has consistently declined to publish a hard count ([TME/PR Newswire, 12 May 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tencent-music-entertainment-group-announces-first-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial-results-302769323.html)).
- **ByteDance's Seed-Music** (Doubao/Seed team, tech report **18 September 2024**) is a unified framework combining an autoregressive LM for generation, **diffusion-based note-level audio editing**, and **zero-shot singing-voice conversion from a 10-second recording** ([ByteDance Seed](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/special/seed-music); [arXiv 2409.09214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09214)).
- **Kunlun Tech / Skywork's Mureka** launched **Mureka O1 and V6 in late March 2025**, marketed as the first "music reasoning" model using chain-of-thought (**MusiCoT**) to draft a song's structure before decoding, and claiming to beat **Suno V4** on mixing quality and vocal texture. Ten languages **including Japanese and Korean**. Kunlun's market cap was ~RMB 43bn (~US$6bn) ([MBW, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/chinas-6b-valued-kunlun-tech-debuts-worlds-first-music-reasoning-model-claims-it-can-outperform-suno/)).

## Contested / two-sided

**1. Is Japan actually a "machine learning paradise"?**
*Claim:* Yes — Art. 30-4 permits commercial training, gives rights holders no opt-out, and imposes no lawful-source requirement, which is broader than the EU/UK/Singapore. Argued by Ueno (who coined the phrase in 2017) and by AI developers who located training operations accordingly.
*Counter:* The label describes only the **input** stage. Output infringement is judged normally, dependence is *presumed* once a model has seen the work, and the 2024 General Understanding says a coexisting "enjoyment" purpose voids the exception entirely. Ueno himself pushes back on the misuse of his phrase. Argued by ACA and by Ueno. ([Ueno / WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf))

**2. Should Article 30-4 be amended to require creator consent?**
*Claim:* Yes. JASRAC and the nine-body Music Industry Council want a statutory right to refuse — "the opportunity to choose" — plus mandatory training-data disclosure and developer liability for infringing output; they note Japan is alone in the G7 in using a non-enjoyment framing.
*Counter:* The Subcommittee's own analysis warns that broadening the proviso to catch style-level substitution "could unduly chill the development of specialised AI models," and that a Diet resolution on the 2018 amendment said acts previously lawful should stay lawful. Also: an opt-out register is only meaningful if models are trained inside Japanese jurisdiction, which most frontier models are not. ([JASRAC](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/aboutus/ai.html) vs [Ueno / WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/documents/d/office-japan/docs-en-tatsuhiro-ueno_general-understanding-on-ai-and-copyright-in-japan_set.pdf))

**3. Does Miku prove audiences will accept AI artists?**
*Claim:* Japan settled this 20 years ago — 580,000+ people have paid to see a projected synthetic singer, and a new voicebank is a commercial event in 2026.
*Counter:* Miku is the opposite case. Her voice is a **licensed sample of one consenting actress**; every song is written by a *human* producer; the value sits in a **pre-cleared derivative-works licence** and a fan community, not in generation. And Crypton itself **stripped licence terms from AI-generated works on piapro** in November 2023. Miku is a precedent for synthetic *instruments* and open licensing, not for prompt-to-song. ([piapro](https://piapro.net/intl/en_for_creators.html); [piapro ch](https://ch.piapro.jp/creator/2023/11/fr2311102))

**4. Is a promotion law with no penalties smart or toothless?**
*Claim (Japanese government):* The AI Promotion Act deliberately avoids penalties to keep Japan attractive to developers while giving the state investigation and guidance powers plus a Cabinet-level strategy HQ.
*Counter:* Korea shipped a real regime with fines and a compute threshold on 22 January 2026; China made labelling legally binding on 1 September 2025. Japan's only lever against a foreign model provider is publicity — which is precisely why CODA had to write a letter rather than file a claim. ([Cabinet Office](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_act/ai_act.html); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1))

**5. Was Supertone's shutdown a verdict on AI voice, or on HYBE's strategy?**
*Claim:* It's a verdict on the product. Roughly US$35m in, KRW 2.3bn of 2025 revenue against a KRW 15.4bn loss, MIDNATT and SYNDI8 both commercially inert. AI voice is a feature, not a company.
*Counter:* Supertone shipped real things — a six-language single in 2023, a 10-second-cloning TTS product in 2025, a voice-conversion system used by the deaf K-pop group Big Ocean. HYBE called it a "realignment of its AI business strategy" and is trying to sell the tools, not bury them. ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/hybe-winds-down-ai-voice-company-supertone-after-investing-nearly-35m/))

**6. Should AI-generated music be labelled by law?**
*Claim:* China already requires it — explicit labels plus metadata on *all* AI output, with platform-level verification — and Korea now requires notification and labelling too. It is enforceable and it happened without collapsing either industry.
*Counter:* China's regime is inseparable from a content-control apparatus most democracies would not import; metadata labels survive exactly one re-encode; and once "AI-assisted" covers Auto-Tune, stem separation, and Synthesizer V vocals, the label stops carrying information. ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1))

## Panel-ready questions

1. **JASRAC wants Article 30-4 amended so creators can refuse AI training. If Japan does that unilaterally and no other G7 country follows, have Japanese creators gained a right — or have Japanese AI companies just lost a market to Silicon Valley while foreign models keep training on J-pop anyway?**
2. **Hatsune Miku has drawn over half a million people to Magical Mirai — and yet Crypton banned licence terms on AI-generated works on its own platform in 2023. Does the Vocaloid ecosystem prove Japan is ready for synthetic artists, or does it prove Japan already found the one model that works, and generative AI is breaking it?**
3. **China made AI labelling mandatory in September 2025 and Korea followed in January 2026. Japan's AI law has no penalties at all. In five years, which of those three will Japanese musicians wish their government had passed?**
4. **HYBE spent 35 million dollars on Supertone and liquidated it in July 2026, weeks after Tencent Music told investors AI songs are a growing share of daily new releases. Is AI voice worthless as a business, or worthless only when you try to sell it as an artist instead of a tool?**
5. **The 2024 Cultural Affairs guidance says training a model exhaustively on one creator to mass-produce their style probably does NOT infringe, as long as no protected expression comes out — but a minority of the committee disagreed loudly enough to be written into the main text. Take a side: if someone ships "the Yoasobi model" tomorrow, has anyone been wronged?**

## Open questions

- **[UNVERIFIED] The ~25,000 public comments** reportedly submitted on the draft General Understanding (Jan–Feb 2024) appears widely in Japanese commentary but I could not confirm it on an ACA page. *Would be settled by:* the Subcommittee's public-comment tabulation on bunka.go.jp.
- **[UNVERIFIED] Whether the Japanese government has actually invoked the AI Promotion Act** against any AI provider. Reporting around the Sora 2 dispute (Oct 2025) said ministers Minoru Kiuchi and Masaaki Taira *warned* it could be used; I found no confirmed invocation. *Would be settled by:* a Cabinet Office or IP Strategy HQ announcement.
- **[UNVERIFIED] Mureka's scale.** Secondary sources cite ~US$12m annualised revenue by November 2025 and ~10 million users by 2026; neither is in a Kunlun Tech filing I could reach. *Would be settled by:* Kunlun Tech's Shenzhen-listed annual report.
- **[UNVERIFIED] Tencent Music's "10 million AI music production users"** figure attributed to the Q4 2025 call — the MBW article carrying it now 404s, and TME's own Q1 2026 release gives no AI user count. Treat as directional only.
- **Conflicting Miku EXPO totals.** Crypton's Sept 2025 release cited 55 cities / 139 shows; its March 2026 release cited 50 cities / 120 concerts. Use Magical Mirai's 580,000 figure instead, which is consistent across both. *Would be settled by:* Crypton's official EXPO tour history page.
- **No confirmed Japanese-major-label licensing deal with a generative music company.** Warner, Universal and Sony have global deals with Klay Vision; I found nothing equivalent from Avex, Sony Music Japan, Victor or Pony Canyon. *Would be settled by:* an Avex or SMEJ IR release.
- **Whether any Japanese court has ruled on Art. 30-4 and generative AI.** As of the ACA's own framing there were "very few" precedents; I found none in music. *Would be settled by:* a decision from the Tokyo District Court IP division.
- **Crypton's explicit policy on third parties training voice models on Miku's voicebank output** is not stated in the English PCL page; the answer likely sits in the Japanese EULA and the キャラクター利用のガイドライン (revised Mar 2026), which I could not fully retrieve.
