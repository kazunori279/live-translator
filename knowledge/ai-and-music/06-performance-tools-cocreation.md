# Performance, tools and human-AI co-creation

**TL;DR for the assistant:** The AI that actually changed music production isn't the song generator — it's stem separation and assistive mixing, now shipping natively inside Ableton Live 12.3, Logic Pro 11, iZotope Ozone 12 and RX 12, Serato and djay. Tracklib's November 2025 survey of 1,734 producers found 32% use AI tools but only 6% use fully generative ones, and 82% oppose text-to-song generation — the industry line isn't "AI vs no AI", it's assistive vs generative. On the performance side, real-time models became genuinely playable in 2025-26: Google's Lyria RealTime went into the Gemini API in May 2025 with ~2-second control latency, and the open-weights Magenta RealTime 2, released 4 June 2026, cut that to about 200 milliseconds running locally on Apple Silicon with MIDI control. Meanwhile the two biggest "virtual performer" successes — ABBA Voyage at £104 million in 2024 and Hatsune Miku's world tours — contain essentially zero generative AI, which is the most interesting fact in this whole area. The unresolved cost: UNESCO projects music creators lose 24% of revenue to generative AI by 2028, and session players, who are work-for-hire and hold no copyright, are first in line.

## Key facts

### Real-time and instrument-shaped models

- **Magenta RealTime** (Google DeepMind/Magenta, [20 June 2025](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/magenta-realtime)) was the first open-weights live music model: 800M-parameter autoregressive transformer trained on ~190k hours of mostly instrumental stock music, generating 2 seconds of audio in 1.25s on a free-tier Colab TPU v2-8 (real-time factor 1.6). Hard limit: a 10-second audio context window, so it cannot reference anything it played earlier — it makes grooves, not song structure.
- **Magenta RealTime 2** ([4 June 2026](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/magenta-realtime-2)) is the significant jump. 2.4B-parameter base model plus a 230M "small" model; frame-level autoregression at **40ms frames** instead of 2-second chunks, giving **~200ms control latency — roughly 15× faster than v1**. Three control modalities: MIDI (including note/drum on-off with an Auto-Strum mode and an attack-accurate mode), audio-embedding style guidance via MusicCoCa, and text prompts.
- MRT2 runs **on device**: a C++ inference engine on Apple's MLX framework. The 230M model streams in real time on any Apple Silicon MacBook including an Air; the 2.4B base model needs an M3 Pro / M2 Max class chip. It ships as a Python library (`pip install magenta-rt`), a standalone app, and an **AUv3 DAW plugin** in the examples directory ([repo](https://github.com/magenta/magenta-realtime)). Code Apache 2.0, weights CC BY 4.0 ([model card](https://huggingface.co/google/magenta-realtime-2)).
- **Lyria RealTime**, the closed cloud sibling, went into the Gemini API and AI Studio at Google I/O in May 2025 and was formally written up [12 June 2025](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/lyria-realtime). 48kHz stereo continuous stream, **maximum ~2 seconds between a control change and hearing it**. Controls: text-prompt blending, tempo, key, per-group instrument muting (drums/bass/other), note-onset density, spectral brightness, and temperature/top-k as a "chaos" knob ([DeepMind model page](https://deepmind.google/models/lyria/lyria-realtime/)). Developer gotcha: density and brightness morph smoothly, but changing BPM or scale is a structural shift requiring `reset_context()`.
- **Toro y Moi (Chaz Bear) played Lyria RealTime live** on a physical MIDI controller before the Google I/O 2025 keynote — a real performance, not a video. MusicFX DJ, the consumer front end, was refined with **Jacob Collier** ([Magenta](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/lyria-realtime)).
- **The Infinite Crate** is the Lyria RealTime VST3/AU/standalone plugin for Mac and Windows ([product page](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/infinite-crate)). It needs a Gemini API key and an internet connection, has **at least 1 second of response latency**, and **caps sessions at 10 minutes** before you must hit Reset. Google open-sourced the whole plugin under Apache 2.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/magenta/the-infinite-crate)) after it was showcased with Daito Manabe in Tokyo and featured at NAMM 2026. Built on JUCE 8 + React.
- **Neutone Morpho** ([neutone.jp/morpho](https://neutone.jp/morpho)) is the practical descendant of IRCAM's RAVE research: real-time neural timbre transfer as a VST3/AU/standalone on macOS 12+ and Windows 10/11. Because it bypasses MIDI pitch/velocity entirely, humming into a violin model is read as bowing and a tongue click becomes a pluck. Free tier keeps 5 models forever; $99 unlocks 27 more; custom model training via Cocoon costs $29–39 per token and the model stays privately owned. Neutone states it trains **only on rights-cleared audio and cites every source in the plugin's model browser** — an unusually specific claim in this field.

### DAW-native AI that producers actually use

- **Ableton Live 12.3** (announced [2 September 2025](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/live-12-3-is-coming/), shipped free to Live 12 owners in November 2025) added **Stem Separation** to Live Suite and Push 3 Standalone: right-click any audio clip, split into vocals / bass / drums / other. It is **powered by algorithms from Music AI, the makers of Moises**, runs **locally with no internet connection**, and offers High Speed vs High Quality modes ([Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/stem-separation-in-ableton-live/)). 12.3 also put the Splice catalogue and "Search with Sound" directly in Live's browser.
- **Ableton Live 12.4** ([5 May 2026](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/live-12-4-is-out-now/)) improved Stem Separation (separate a selected region; merge stems back onto one track) and added Link Audio for streaming audio between devices over LAN. **Move 2.0 and Note 2.0** gained audio recording and warping. Worth saying out loud on a panel: Live's five **MIDI "Generator" tools are algorithmic — probability and rules, not trained models.** The word "generative" in Ableton's marketing does not mean machine learning.
- **Logic Pro 11 / Logic Pro for iPad 2** (Apple, announced [7 May 2024](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/logic-pro-takes-music-making-to-the-next-level-with-new-ai-features/), shipped 13 May 2024) introduced **Session Players** — extending Drummer to a Bass Player and Keyboard Player, trained with real bassists — plus **Stem Splitter** and ChromaGlow. Stem Splitter *requires* M-series Apple silicon. Session Players follow the project's global Chord track so multiple players lock together ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro/session-players-overview-lgcpbf624405/mac)). Free update for existing owners; $199.99 new on Mac, $4.99/month on iPad.
- **iZotope Ozone 12** (3 September 2025) added **Stem EQ** — separate EQ curves for vocals, drums, bass and instruments *inside a finished stereo master*, with no access to the original session — plus ML-driven **Bass Control** and **Unlimiter** (restores transients to over-squashed audio) and IRC 5 limiting. Master Assistant gained a "Custom" flow: pick genre targets, toggle modules, set your own LUFS ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/products/ozone-advanced)). iZotope's own framing is "intelligent tech that guides, not decides."
- **iZotope RX 12** (spring 2026; iZotope's own [what's-new post](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/rx-12) is dated 14 May 2026, trade press covered the launch 29 April 2026) added **Scene Rebalance** — pull dialogue, music and effects apart from a single stereo render, Advanced tier only — and **Stems View**, so the full RX toolkit applies to one isolated stem. Music Rebalance and Dialogue Isolate now run in real time on rebuilt neural nets. Elements $99 / Standard $399 / Advanced $1,399.
- **Sonible** ships a six-plugin [smart:bundle](https://www.sonible.com/smartbundle/) (smart:EQ 4, smart:comp 3, smart:limit, smart:reverb 2, smart:deess, smart:gate) at €399. Note for accuracy: **there is no smart:EQ 5** as of mid-2026 — smart:EQ 4 is still current; the 2025-26 releases were smart:comp 3 (Dec 2025), an SSL autoSeries collaboration (Jan 2026), free puffer:fish (Feb 2026) and pure:level (Mar 2026).
- **Splice** announced three generative features on [15 April 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/splice-launches-ai-tools-that-compensate-sample-creators-when-their-sounds-are-used/): **Variations** (new versions of any catalogue sample with altered structure/key/BPM), **Craft** (turn a sample into a playable instrument inside Splice INSTRUMENT) and **Magic Fit** (adapt any sound to the session's harmony and rhythm, summer 2026). The headline is the economics: **the original sample creator is paid both when their sound is used as a source and each time a variation is downloaded**, across a 3M+ human-made licensed catalogue. Splice acquired Spitfire Audio (April 2025) and voice platform Kits AI (January 2026) ([Splice newsfeed](https://splice.com/blog/newsfeed/)).
- **Suno Studio** launched [25 September 2025](https://suno.com/blog/suno-studio) as a "generative audio workstation" on the $30/mo Premier tier: multitrack timeline, generate stem variations that flow with existing audio, and **export both stems and MIDI back into a conventional DAW**. Built partly on the engineering team from Suno's June 2025 acquisition of browser DAW WavTool. Practical caveat producers report: Suno's deliberately human timing drift means you should lock BPM *before* exporting stems or they will walk off the grid by bar 32.
- **Moises / Music AI** is the infrastructure behind much of this: [70 million users, 15 million new in 2025, 1.2 billion minutes of audio processed, $50M+ raised](https://moises.ai/newsroom/company-milestones/2025-year-in-review/), 33 languages, 190 countries. Its stem separation powers Ableton Live. Its AI Studio launched August 2025 and lifted daily new users 30%.
- **DJ software**: Algoriddim's **djay Pro Neural Mix** got AudioShake's models in djay Pro 5.0 ([6 December 2023](https://www.audioshake.ai/post/algoriddim-djaypro-neural-mix)) — the first time AudioShake stems ran in real time on both mobile and desktop, optimised for Apple's Neural Engine. djay's distinctive move is **stem-mapped EQ**: the low/mid/high knobs control drums/harmonics/vocals instead of frequency bands. Serato Stems and rekordbox 7 followed; Traktor Pro 4 pre-renders instead.
- **Koala Sampler** ($4.99, iOS and Android) has had one-tap AI **Split Stems** since v1.4055 in [May 2022](https://musictech.com/news/gear/koala-sampler-app-separates-stems-in-one-tap/) — a reminder that "AI in the workflow" reached a five-dollar phone app four years ago.

### Live performance and virtual performers

- **Holly Herndon's Holly+** is the reference case for artist-controlled voice models: built with Herndon Dryhurst Studio, Never Before Heard Sounds and Voctro Labs, with a **real-time version premiered at Sónar 2021 — described as the first live machine-learning "identity play" performance** ([Ars Electronica](https://ars.electronica.art/planetb/en/holly-plus/)). It won the STARTS Prize Grand Prize in 2022. The economics matter as much as the tech: a DAO of token holders votes on approved uses and revenue splits **50% creator / 40% DAO treasury / 10% Herndon**.
- **ABBA Voyage is the non-AI benchmark, and it is enormous.** £104.34m revenue in 2024, **1.06 million tickets across 374 performances** in a purpose-built 3,000-seat arena, >90% occupancy, and an estimated **£1.51 billion economic impact on London** since opening in May 2022. Pre-tax profit still fell 30.5% to £4.22m ([MBW, 1 October 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/abba-voyage-generated-113m-in-2024-as-demand-for-virtual-concert-series-stayed-strong-in-third-year/)). The avatars are motion capture and VFX, not generative AI, and the band is live humans.
- **Hatsune Miku** is the older precedent — a Crypton Future Media singing-synthesis product turned touring act, with a live human band behind the screen. **MIKU EXPO 2026 North America ran 12 April – 19 May 2026 across 15 cities** ([mikuexpo.com](https://mikuexpo.com/na2026/)). Also not generative AI: concatenative/sample-based vocal synthesis, fan-written songs, human motion capture — and since 2024 the "hologram" is an LED wall rather than a Pepper's-ghost projection, a change fans openly complained about.
- **Live visuals and lighting** are converging on the same pattern as audio: AI busks the atmosphere layer, humans lock the critical looks. TouchDesigner remains the standard for audio-reactive generative visuals, and because AI lighting systems output plain DMX or sACN they drop into existing rigs. The disciplined workflow described by lighting practitioners: let the system improvise back-beams and pixel-map layers, then **record anything good into a cue and freeze it in the show file**.

### Accessibility

- Accessibility became a headline programming pillar at NAMM 2026, where the MIDI Association's **Music Accessibility Standard Special Interest Group (MASSIG)** presented alongside MIDI 2.0 work. MIDI 2.0's bidirectional communication, Profiles and Property Exchange are being positioned as the substrate for assistive tech ([MIDI Association, May 2026](https://midi.org/2026-music-accessibility-webinar)). Named work includes Arcana Strum (adaptive instrument), AptiPlay (a MIDI 2.0 controller taking up to eight switch/lever inputs) and a Bettermaker collaboration on voice control and AI-assisted control of pro audio hardware.
- **Drake Music** (UK, "leaders in music, disability & technology") maintains the practical catalogue: Skoog/Skwitch, Soundbeam, Clarion (playable with any body part), Jamboxx (breath-controlled), Apollo Ensemble, Subpac, LinnStrument, Mini Mu gloves ([resource list](https://www.drakemusic.org/technology/resources-links/other-accessible-music-tech/)). Honest framing for a panel: **most of the accessibility wins here are interface, protocol and hardware design — not machine learning.** AI's clearest contributions so far are screen-reader/voice control, stem separation for practice, and lowering the technical floor for composition.

### Game and film scoring

- Adaptive game music remains a **middleware** problem, not a model problem. Wwise (free under $150K project revenue) and FMOD (free under $200K) still own AAA and indie respectively; Unreal's MetaSounds is the procedural challenger, synthesising audio in-engine rather than triggering files. The 2026 consensus across game-audio guides: **generative models produce material, middleware produces interactivity** — you still author states, transitions on bar/beat boundaries, and RTPC curves by hand.

## Contested / two-sided

**1. "AI is already mainstream in music production" — 87% vs 32% vs 12%.**
LANDR's November 2025 study says [87% of music makers use AI somewhere](https://www.landr.com/ai). Tracklib's independent-ish producer panel says [32% use any AI tool and only 6% use generative ones](https://musictech.com/news/industry/music-producers-are-rejecting-ai-study-reveals-over-80-of-producers-are-against-ai-generated-songs/). MIDiA's creator survey puts AI co-pilot use in the low double digits. **Counter-argument:** LANDR is an AI-mastering company surveying its own subscribers, and "used AI somewhere" sweeps in cover art and promo copy. The defensible line for a panel is that *assistive* AI is near-universal and *generative* AI is a single-digit minority — and anyone quoting one number without the other is selling something.

**2. Assistive AI is fine, generative AI is theft — is that line coherent?**
Producers overwhelmingly accept stem separation, EQ assistants and mastering assistants; 82% reject text-to-song. **Counter:** iZotope's Stem EQ and RX's Scene Rebalance let you pull apart and re-EQ *someone else's finished master* without their session files, and Ableton put four-way separation into a mainstream DAW. Those are enormously powerful appropriation tools. The distinction producers are actually drawing may be less about ethics than about **who keeps the job** — the tool that speeds up my work is good, the tool that replaces me is theft.

**3. Do these tools actually save time, or just move it?**
The claimed wins are real and specific: a stereo master you can re-balance, a bass part in 30 seconds, stems from a reference track for a remix. **Counter:** the reported friction is also specific — Suno stems drift off the grid unless you lock BPM before export; The Infinite Crate dies at 10 minutes and lags a full second; RAVE-family plugins have latency that's fine in the studio and dicey on stage; game composers still hand-author every Wwise transition. A useful panel provocation: *the demo is 30 seconds, the workflow is 30 hours.*

**4. Are real-time models instruments, or slot machines?**
Google's explicit framing for Lyria RealTime is "music as a verb" and putting the human deeper in the loop; Toro y Moi played one live at I/O 2025; MRT2's 200ms MIDI latency is inside the range where a player can entrain to it. **Counter:** an instrument gives you *repeatable* cause and effect. Magenta RealTime v1 literally cannot remember what it played 11 seconds ago, and Lyria requires a context reset to change key or tempo. Critics argue you are steering a weather system, not playing an instrument — closer to conducting a jam you don't control than to a violin.

**5. Session Players and generative stems vs actual session musicians.**
Apple trained Bass Player "in collaboration with top bass players"; Splice pays sample creators every time a Variation is generated or downloaded — evidence that vendors can build consent and compensation in. **Counter:** UNESCO projects [24% revenue loss for music creators by 2028](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/creators-face-projected-global-revenue-losses-24-2028-new-unesco-report-shows), and session players are structurally the most exposed group: work-for-hire, union scale per session, no copyright interest, therefore no seat at the table when labels license catalogues for AI training. The AFM has litigated exactly this against Universal and Warner over their Suno and Udio deals. [UNVERIFIED — I could not fetch the AFM complaint or a primary court docket within this session; treat the litigation detail as reported-not-confirmed.]

**6. Virtual performance proves audiences will accept synthetic performers.**
ABBA Voyage did £104m in 2024 at >90% occupancy; Miku fills arenas across 15 North American cities. **Counter — and this is the strongest point in the whole file:** neither uses generative AI. Voyage is motion capture of the four real members with a live human band; Miku is sample-based vocal synthesis singing songs written by named human fans, with a live human band. What audiences bought was *authored spectacle plus a human community*, not machine autonomy. Nobody has yet demonstrated a paying arena audience for a performer with no human authorship behind it.

**7. Accessibility: liberation or marketing?**
AI lowers the technical floor — voice control, auto-accompaniment, stem separation for practice, composition assistance for people with cognitive or motor disabilities. **Counter:** the concrete 2026 wins named by the MIDI Association's accessibility SIG and Drake Music are **protocol and hardware** — MIDI 2.0 Profiles and Property Exchange, switch-input controllers, breath and eye-gaze instruments, screen-reader-friendly software. Disabled musicians' most common complaint is not "no AI" but "your plugin GUI has no keyboard navigation." A panel should press on whether AI budgets are displacing basic accessible-design budgets.

## Panel-ready questions

1. **Stem separation is now a right-click inside Ableton Live and a $4.99 phone app.** Every producer says it's the acceptable face of AI — but it also means anyone can extract a clean acapella or an isolated bassline from any finished record ever released. Why is that ethically different from a generative model trained on the same record?

2. **Tracklib found 82% of producers oppose text-to-song generation, but 32% happily use AI stem splitters, EQ and mastering assistants.** Is that a principled distinction, or is it just "AI that helps me is fine, AI that replaces me is theft"? Where exactly does the line sit — and does Splice's pay-the-sample-creator-per-variation model dissolve it?

3. **ABBA Voyage grossed £104 million and Hatsune Miku toured 15 North American cities, and neither of them uses generative AI at all.** Does that mean the market for synthetic performance is really a market for *human authorship wrapped in spectacle* — and that a fully AI-authored arena act would flop?

4. **Magenta RealTime 2 got control latency down to about 200 milliseconds on a laptop with MIDI in.** At what latency does a generative model stop being a toy and start being an instrument? And can something be an instrument if you can't make it do the same thing twice?

5. **UNESCO projects a 24% revenue hit for music creators by 2028, and session players — work-for-hire, no copyright, no royalty — are first in line.** Apple trained Logic's Bass Player with real bassists and Splice pays creators per use. Is per-use compensation an adequate answer, or does it just convert a career into a micropayment?

## Try this tonight

- **Free, no install:** open Google AI Studio's Lyria RealTime demos (PromptDJ MIDI lets you map real MIDI hardware to text-prompt weights via WebMIDI) — see [magenta.withgoogle.com/lyria-realtime](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/lyria-realtime).
- **Free, in your DAW:** download [The Infinite Crate](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/infinite-crate) (VST3/AU/standalone, Mac + Windows), grab a free Gemini API key, and record its output onto an audio track. Remember the 10-minute reset.
- **Free, on your laptop, offline:** `uv pip install "magenta-rt[mlx]"` then `mrt models init` — the 230M model streams in real time on any Apple Silicon Mac ([repo](https://github.com/magenta/magenta-realtime)).
- **Already paid for:** if you own Live 12 Suite, right-click any audio clip → Stem Separation. If you own Logic Pro 11 on Apple silicon, try Stem Splitter on a mono reference, then drop a Bass Player region over your Chord track.
- **Under $100:** [Neutone Morpho](https://neutone.jp/morpho) free tier (5 models, keep forever) — sing into a violin model and hear pitch/loudness resynthesised as bowing.
- **On your phone:** [Koala Sampler](https://musictech.com/news/gear/koala-sampler-app-separates-stems-in-one-tap/), $4.99, one-tap Split Stems on any recording.

- **How much of Ableton's Stem Separation adoption is remix work on other people's masters?** Ableton and Moises publish user counts but no breakdown of use case. Only telemetry or a targeted survey would resolve it.
