# Industry economics, labels and platforms

**TL;DR for the assistant:** Follow the money and you get a paradox. Deezer says more than half of everything uploaded to it daily — about 90,000 tracks a day as of June 2026 — is now fully AI-generated, yet those tracks pull only 1 to 3 percent of actual streams, and Goldman Sachs estimates AI music is still only about 0.1 percent of the label royalty pool. Meanwhile the majors have flipped from suing to signing: Universal settled with Udio in October 2025, Warner settled with Suno in November 2025, and all three majors licensed the startup Klay — with Sony the lone holdout still in court. The real money question is not "will AI songs out-earn humans", it is who gets paid from those settlements: the American Federation of Musicians sued Universal and Warner in June 2026 saying session players were licensed into AI training with no consent, credit or cheque. And the most exposed corner of the business is not pop stardom, it is production and library music, which CISAC projects could be roughly 60 percent gen-AI by 2028. And as of four days ago the industry has stopped arguing and started legislating: on 29 July 2026 eleven companies — the three majors plus Believe, BMG, Concord, Dirty Hit, Glassnote, HYBE, Mom+Pop and Partisan — proposed global chart-eligibility principles for AI recordings, and IFPI rolled them out worldwide on 30 July, requiring that a charting record be "substantially human made," made with an authorised AI service, and labelled as AI to consumers.

## Key facts

### Deezer — the only platform publishing hard detection numbers

- **21 July 2026: Deezer said fully AI-generated tracks exceeded 50% of all daily new-music uploads for the first time**, peaking in June 2026 at a monthly average of ~90,000 tracks a day ([Deezer newsroom, 21 Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/); [TechCrunch, 21 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/music-streamer-deezer-says-more-than-50-of-daily-uploads-are-ai-generated/)).
- **The growth curve, all from Deezer's own detector:** ~10,000/day in Jan 2025 → 20,000 (18%) Apr 2025 → 30,000 (28%) Sep 2025 → 50,000 (34%) Nov 2025 → 60,000 (39%) Jan 2026 → 75,000 (44%) Apr 2026 → 90,000 (>50%) Jun 2026. Deezer detected and tagged **13.4 million** fully AI tracks during 2025 alone ([Deezer, Jan 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/01/deezer-confirms-demonetization-of-ai-music/); [Deezer, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)).
- **Supply ≠ demand.** Fully AI-generated music is only **1–3% of total streams** on Deezer, because detected tracks are auto-excluded from editorial playlists and algorithmic recommendations ([Deezer, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)).
- **Up to 85% of streams on fully AI-generated tracks were fraudulent in 2025**, versus ~8% fraud across Deezer's whole catalogue. Deezer demonetised them — fraudulent streams are stripped out of the royalty pool before payout ([Deezer, Jan 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/01/deezer-confirms-demonetization-of-ai-music/)).
- **New delisting policy (Jul 2026):** Deezer will remove any AI track it *suspects* was used in streaming fraud, plus any AI track with no streams for 6+ months. CEO Alexis Lanternier: "we are taking additional steps to safeguard the rights of artists and songwriters, while maintaining focus on music that fans actually love" ([Deezer, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)).
- **Detection is now a product.** Deezer's patent-pending detector (live since Jan 2025, claimed 99.8% accuracy on Suno/Udio-class models) has been sold to other platforms, CMOs and distributors since Jan 2026; Billboard uses it to identify AI titles on its charts ([Deezer, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/); [Deezer AI labelling page](https://www.deezer.com/explore/en-us/features/ai-labelling/)).
- **Listeners can't tell.** In a Deezer/Ipsos survey of ~9,000 people across 8 countries, **97% could not identify the fully AI-generated track** in a blind test; **80% wanted AI content clearly labelled** ([Deezer, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)).

### Spotify — disclosure, not detection

- **25 September 2025: Spotify's three-pillar AI policy.** (1) A new impersonation policy — unauthorised AI voice clones and vocal impersonation banned unless the impersonated artist authorises it; (2) a music spam filter targeting mass uploads, duplicates, SEO hacks and artificially short tracks; (3) support for a **DDEX** industry standard for disclosing AI use in credits ([Spotify newsroom, 25 Sep 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/)).
- **Spotify removed over 75 million "spammy" tracks in the 12 months to September 2025** ([Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/); [MBW, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-has-deleted-75m-spammy-tracks-as-it-unveils-new-ai-music-policies/)).
- **18 distributor/label-services partners signed up to the DDEX disclosure standard at launch** — Amuse, AudioSalad, Believe, CD Baby, DistroKid, Downtown, EMPIRE, EmuBands, EMS, FUGA, IDOL, Kontor, Labelcamp, NueMeta, Revelator, RouteNote, SonoSuite, Soundrop. **None of the three majors were on the initial list** ([Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/); [Music Ally, 25 Sep 2025](https://musically.com/2025/09/25/spotify-reveals-its-latest-measures-to-handle-ai-music/)).
- **Spotify's AI Credits are voluntary and near-invisible.** Per Spotify's own artist support page: credits are "currently in beta", "only visible to listeners on mobile", submitted through "certain distributors", cover lyrics / vocals / instrumental performances / production, and "don't appear on artist profiles or as track-level labels." They are explicitly "optional" ([Spotify for Artists: AI credits](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/ai-credits/)). Reported beta start date of 16 April 2026 is [UNVERIFIED] on a primary Spotify page.
- **Spotify frames disclosure as non-punitive** — it says it is "not about punishing artists who use AI responsibly or down ranking tracks for disclosing information about how they were made" ([TechCrunch, 25 Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/spotify-updates-ai-policy-to-label-tracks-cut-down-on-spam/)). This is the opposite of Deezer, which demotes and demonetises.
- **Spotify paid the music industry over $11 billion in 2025**, up more than 10% YoY, taking all-time payouts to nearly $70 billion; roughly half of royalties were generated by independent artists and labels ([Loud & Clear, 11 Mar 2026](https://loudandclear.byspotify.com/takeaways/); [Spotify newsroom, 11 Mar 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-03-11/loud-and-clear-music-economics-highlights/)).

### Apple Music, YouTube, and the rest

- **4 March 2026: Apple Music introduced "Transparency Tags"** across four categories — Artwork (album level), Track, Composition, Music Video. Crucially they are **self-declared by labels and distributors**, currently optional with "if omitted, none is assumed" in the spec, and Apple says they will be required on new deliveries in future. Apple: "Proper tagging of content is the first step in giving the music industry the data and tools needed to develop thoughtful policies around AI" ([MBW, Mar 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/apple-music-launches-ai-transparency-tags-but-only-if-labels-and-distributors-choose-to-declare-them/); [Forbes, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/03/05/apple-music-introduces-transparency-tags-to-flag-ai-generated-music-and-artwork/)).
- **YouTube has moved from self-disclosure to automatic detection and labelling of AI content**, and extended its Content-ID-style **likeness detection** (launched to a limited creator group in October 2025) to celebrities and agencies including CAA, UTA and WME in April 2026 ([MBW on auto-labelling](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-will-now-automatically-detect-and-label-ai-videos-even-when-creators-dont-disclose-it/)).
- **YouTube's Dream Track (Nov 2023)** — 30-second AI vocal clones of nine opted-in artists (Charli XCX, John Legend, Sia, T-Pain, Troye Sivan, Demi Lovato, Charlie Puth, Alec Benjamin, Papoose), watermarked with DeepMind's SynthID, released to ~100 US creators. It is the template for every "opt-in voice licensing" deal since ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/ai-and-music-experiment/)).
- **There is no industry consensus.** Deezer detects and demonetises; Spotify and Apple rely on rightsholder self-disclosure; Bandcamp reportedly bans fully AI tracks and Tidal has cut monetisation ([Music Ally, 21 Jul 2026](https://musically.com/2026/07/21/ai-generated-music-is-now-more-than-half-of-deezers-uploads/)). Amazon Music has no comparable published AI disclosure standard [UNVERIFIED].

### Labels: from litigation to licensing

- **29–30 October 2025: UMG settled its copyright suit against Udio** and announced a licensed AI music creation and streaming platform for 2026, artists and songwriters participating **opt-in**, creations confined to a "walled garden" with fingerprinting and filtering; Udio immediately removed users' ability to download their own tracks, with a 48-hour grace window ([PR Newswire / UMG-Udio, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/universal-music-group-and-udio-announce-udios-first-strategic-agreements-for-new-licensed-ai-music-creation-platform-302599129.html); [Music Ally, 30 Oct 2025](https://musically.com/2025/10/30/umg-settles-udio-lawsuit-companies-plan-new-ai-music-service-together/)).
- **30 October 2025: UMG and Stability AI announced a strategic alliance** to co-develop "professional" AI music creation tools trained on licensed catalogues. UMG's Michael Nash: UMG "will only consider advancing AI tools and products based on models that are trained responsibly" ([Stability AI, 30 Oct 2025](https://stability.ai/news-updates/universal-music-group-and-stability-ai-announce-strategic-alliance)).
- **25 November 2025: Warner Music settled with Suno and licensed its catalogue.** Suno agreed to retire its current models and launch licensed replacements in 2026, gate downloads behind paid tiers with caps, and let Warner artists opt in to voice/likeness/style generation for compensation. Warner also **sold Songkick to Suno** ([MBW, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-settles-with-suno-strikes-first-of-its-kind-deal-with-ai-song-generator/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/warner-music-group-settles-ai-infringement-suit-with-suno-1236435516/)).
- **No financial terms of the Warner–Suno deal are public.** Suno has fought in court to keep the settlement terms away from UMG and Sony, arguing litigation-driven deals are poor evidence of market rates; UMG and Sony counter that it is a genuine forward-looking training licence ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-fights-to-keep-warner-music-settlement-terms-away-from-umg-and-sony/)). **Nobody outside the parties knows the per-generation or per-stream economics.**
- **20 November 2025: Klay Vision signed recorded-music and publishing licences with all three majors** — UMG/UMPG, Sony Music/Sony Music Publishing, WMG/Warner Chappell — making it the first AI music company to do so. Klay is building a subscription "interactive streaming" product on a licensed Large Music Model; team includes ex-Sony digital president Thomas Hesse and ex-Spotify/Echo Nest scientist Brian Whitman ([MBW, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/umg-sony-and-warner-strike-licensing-deals-with-new-ai-music-platform-klay/); [Klay press page](https://klay.vision/press)).
- **UMG has also done AI deals with YouTube, Meta, TikTok, KDDI, BandLab, SoundLabs and ProRata.** The ProRata agreement (Aug 2024) is about *attribution* — measuring which sources contributed to an AI output and splitting payment fractionally ([Music Ally, 7 Aug 2024](https://musically.com/2024/08/07/ai-attribution-startup-prorata-raises-25m-and-signs-umg-deal/)).
- **Sony is the holdout.** It is still litigating against Suno in Massachusetts and Udio in SDNY. A judge denied Sony's bid to add 30,442 recordings to the Udio case, so Sony filed a fresh SDNY complaint over ~30,117 tracks found in Udio's training data. Sony/UMG moved to expand the Suno case from 560 works to 61,026 ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sony-music-denied-bid-to-add-30k-recordings-to-its-udio-lawsuit/); [Variety, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/sony-music-new-lawsuit-udio-ai-music-generator-1236817746/)). Sony CEO Rob Stringer's line: direct licensing in advance of launching products is "the only appropriate way to build them" ([Hype Magazine on Sony investor day, Jun 2025](https://www.thehypemagazine.com/2025/06/13/sony-music-ceo-talks-ai-we-are-going-to-do-deals-for-new-music-ai-products-this-year/)).
- **In Germany, Suno lost.** The Munich Regional Court ruled Suno breached copyright by training on GEMA's repertoire; Suno disagreed and is weighing appeal ([Variety, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/suno-loses-ai-lawsuit-gema-1236825010/)). Any US fair-use merits ruling has slipped: the Massachusetts scheduling order sets dispositive motions for **9 April 2027**.

### Who is *not* getting paid: the AFM suit

- **5 June 2026: the American Federation of Musicians sued UMG and WMG in SDNY**, alleging the majors licensed member session recordings to Suno and Udio without the compensation, credit or consent required by the Sound Recording Labor Agreement's "new use" clause (Article 21(a)), and refused to disclose which recordings went into training sets ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/musicians-union-sues-umg-and-warner-music-alleging-member-recordings-were-licensed-to-suno-and-udio-without-compensation-or-credit/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/musicians-union-lawsuit-ai-song-generator-settlement-1236614835/)).
- **The labels' defence:** UMG says Article 21(a) is "a rate-conversion provision, not an open-ended royalty provision"; Warner says there is no destination rate to convert *to* because no AFM AI-training rate has ever been negotiated, and calls the suit "an improper attempt to place a judicial thumb on the negotiation scales." An amended complaint naming Warner Records and Atlantic was filed **24 July 2026** ([MBW, Jul 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-musicians-union-files-amended-lawsuit-against-universal-and-warner-over-suno-and-udio-ai-deals/)).
- The SRLA expired **31 January 2026**; successor negotiations began in LA in January 2026 with AI consent/compensation/credit as the AFM's headline demand.

### Fraud: the Michael Smith case

- **September 2024: SDNY indicted Michael Smith** of Cornelius, NC on wire fraud conspiracy, wire fraud and money laundering conspiracy — hundreds of thousands of AI-generated songs streamed billions of times by bots, allegedly netting **over $10 million** in royalties ([DOJ, Sep 2024](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/north-carolina-musician-charged-music-streaming-fraud-aided-artificial-intelligence)).
- **19 March 2026: Smith pleaded guilty** to one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud (5-year max, down from a combined 60) and agreed to forfeit **$8,091,843.64** ([DOJ, Mar 2026](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/north-carolina-man-pleads-guilty-music-streaming-fraud-aided-artificial-intelligence-0)).
- **The mechanics matter for the panel:** Smith ran 1,000+ bot accounts, targeted roughly **661,440 streams/day** (~$1.2m/yr), and deliberately spread plays thinly across a very large AI-generated catalogue so no single track tripped anomaly detection. The scheme ran ~2017–2023 and was surfaced with help from the **Mechanical Licensing Collective**. AI's role here was not making hits — it was manufacturing *cover* ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/north-carolina-man-pleads-guilty-music-streaming-fraud-aided-artificial-intelligence-0)).
- **Sentencing was scheduled for 29 July 2026** before Judge John G. Koeltl. **[UNVERIFIED] — the actual sentence handed down is not confirmed in any source I could retrieve.** Do not state a prison term.
- IFPI CEO Victoria Oakley's framing, March 2026: "Streaming fraud is theft, plain and simple" ([IFPI GMR 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)).

### The fake-band episodes

- **The Velvet Sundown (Jun–Jul 2025):** a "70s psych rock" act with invented members went from ~325,000 Spotify monthly listeners in late June to over 1 million by 5 July and peaked around 1.4 million. A Canadian using the pseudonym "Andrew Frelon" posed as its spokesperson — a hoax on top of a hoax that drove roughly 700,000 of those listeners — before the project admitted being a "synthetic music project guided by human creative direction," made with Suno ([CBC, 2025](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/ai-band-hoax-velvet-sundown-1.7575874); [Berklee explainer](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-now/news/velvet-sundown-ai-band-controversy)). Growth came from playlist stuffing feeding Discover Weekly, not bots.
- **Breaking Rust (Nov 2025):** "Walk My Walk" became the first AI-generated country track to top Billboard's **Country Digital Song Sales** chart. The deflating detail: per Luminate it took about **2,500 paid downloads**. That chart excludes streaming and radio ([NPR, 10 Nov 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/10/nx-s1-5604320/breaking-rust-is-a-hot-new-country-act-on-the-billboard-charts-its-powered-by-ai); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Rust)). Creator Aubierre Rivaldo Taylor has no BMI or ASCAP registration for the act.
- **Xania Monet (Sep–Nov 2025):** Mississippi poet Telisha "Nikki" Jones writes the lyrics, Suno generates the music. Bidding for the project reportedly reached **$3 million**; she signed to Hallwood Media (ex-Interscope's Neil Jacobson). She became the first AI act to chart on a Billboard *radio airplay* chart — No. 30 on Adult R&B Airplay, dated 11 Nov 2025 — with 9.8 million US on-demand streams by mid-September. Kehlani and SZA publicly objected ([Forbes, 27 Sep 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/dougmelville/2025/09/27/al-singer-xania-monet-just-charted-on-billboard-signed-3m-deal-is-this-the-future-of-music/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xania_Monet)).

### Chart eligibility: the industry wrote the rulebook, 29–30 July 2026

- **29 July 2026: eleven music companies published a proposal for "Global Principles for Eligibility of Recordings Developed Using AI in Official Music Charts."** Signatories: **Sony Music, Universal Music Group, Warner Music Group, Believe, BMG, Concord, Dirty Hit, Glassnote, HYBE, Mom+Pop and Partisan** — all three majors plus the largest indies and Korea's HYBE ([MBW, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/majors-believe-bmg-concord-hybe-and-more-propose-new-chart-eligibility-rules-for-ai-music/)). Their framing: draw "a clear boundary between human-led creativity and works that are purely synthetic or generated by unauthorized AI models."
- **30 July 2026: IFPI adopted them and began rolling them out worldwide** ([IFPI, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-rolls-out-global-principles-for-the-eligibility-of-recordings-developed-using-ai-in-official-music-charts-worldwide/)). **This is four days old — it supersedes any "the charts haven't decided" framing.**
- **The six tests. A recording made with generative AI is chart-eligible only if *all* of these hold:** (1) any gen-AI services used were **properly authorised and lawful**; (2) the recording is **"substantially human made"**; (3) it raises **no manipulation concerns**; (4) it complies with **copyright, related rights and personality rights**; (5) making it available does **not breach the AI service's terms**; (6) the AI use is **appropriately signalled to consumers on downstream services** per applicable law and industry standards ([IFPI, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-rolls-out-global-principles-for-the-eligibility-of-recordings-developed-using-ai-in-official-music-charts-worldwide/)).
- **Where it bites first:** charts IFPI directly manages — the Official MENA Charts, Official Southeast Asia Charts, the Official South Africa Chart and several Latin American charts — with IFPI working to extend it to **20+ further official chart programmes**, named as including Australia's ARIA, France's SNEP, Germany's Offizielle Deutsche Charts and South Korea's Circle Chart ([MBW, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/ifpi-rolls-out-labels-ai-chart-eligibility-principles-across-its-global-network-of-official-music-charts/)).
- IFPI CEO **Victoria Oakley, 30 July 2026**: "Official music charts do more than just track sales; they celebrate human artistry and endeavour" — paired with the pro-AI half of the message, that "when properly licensed and authorised, AI provides exciting opportunities to enhance the creative toolkit" ([IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-rolls-out-global-principles-for-the-eligibility-of-recordings-developed-using-ai-in-official-music-charts-worldwide/)). The labels' own line: these principles "would enable official charts to accommodate appropriate use of AI in the creative process whilst remaining an authentic celebration of human artistry."
- **This is the second AI move from the same bodies in three weeks.** On 10 July 2026 IFPI, RIAA, A2IM, WIN, IMPALA, the Recording Academy and SAG-AFTRA launched a voluntary track-level gen-AI labelling programme ([IFPI, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/music-community-introduces-new-labelling-program-to-distinguish-generative-ai-in-sound-recordings/)). Principle 6 is what turns that voluntary label into a **condition of charting** — the disclosure regime now has teeth.
- **Not the same thing as Billboard.** These are IFPI-network official charts. **[UNVERIFIED] — I found no statement from Billboard, Luminate or the UK's Official Charts Company adopting the principles**, and the Breaking Rust / Xania Monet chart entries were on Billboard charts, which sit outside IFPI's direct control.

### The most exposed segment: sync, library and production music

- **Sync was the only IFPI revenue line to fall in 2025: down 2.0% to US$641 million**, after four consecutive years of growth ([MBW on IFPI GMR 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/global-recorded-music-revenues-hit-31-7bn-in-2025-up-6-4-yoy-users-of-paid-music-subscriptions-reach-837m/)). Correlation, not proven causation — but it is the number to quote.
- **CISAC projects gen-AI music will be ~60% of music *library* revenues by 2028** — versus ~20% of streaming platform revenues. Library and production music is the single most exposed category in the study ([CISAC/PMP Strategy, 2 Dec 2024](https://www.cisac.org/Newsroom/news-releases/global-economic-study-shows-human-creators-future-risk-generative-ai)).
- **Epidemic Sound has pivoted rather than defended.** In April 2026 it announced expansion beyond a pure catalogue/licensing model into a "next-generation creative platform" with AI workflow tools, positioning licensed human catalogue plus AI adaptation against "AI-only" providers ([Epidemic Sound, Apr 2026](https://news.cision.com/epidemic-sound/r/epidemic-sound-expands-beyond-music-catalog-as-it-builds-the-next-generation-creative-platform,c4338730)); in May 2026 it launched an enterprise "soundtracking playbook" aimed at brands worried about AI training-data provenance and rights ([Epidemic Sound, May 2026](https://corporate.epidemicsound.com/press-and-media/press-releases/2026/epidemic-sound-launches-the-enterprise-soundtracking-playbook-to-help-brands-navigate-a-more-complex-music-licensing-landscape/)).

### Creator income forecasts

- **CISAC / PMP Strategy (2 Dec 2024): 24% of music creators' revenues at risk by 2028**, a cumulative **€10 billion** loss in music over five years (€22bn including audiovisual). Gen-AI music providers' revenues forecast to go from **€0.1bn (2023) to €4bn (2028)**; the wider gen-AI music+AV output market from ~€3bn to €64bn ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/Newsroom/news-releases/global-economic-study-shows-human-creators-future-risk-generative-ai)).
- **Goldmedia for Sacem and GEMA (Jan 2024): 27% of authors' revenue at risk in the medium term**, a cumulative **€2.7 billion** loss for Sacem and GEMA members by 2028. 71% of surveyed creators believed AI would deprive them of income; 35% already used AI (51% of under-35s); 64% said the risks outweigh the opportunities ([CISAC summary of the Sacem/GEMA study](https://www.cisac.org/Newsroom/society-news/sacem-and-gema-unveil-results-study-impact-artificial-intelligence-music)).
- **Contrast with the sell side:** Goldman Sachs' *Music in the Air* puts **direct AI music revenue at ~$2.1 billion by 2030** (from ~$400m in 2024) and says "we have not yet seen a material impact on the label revenue pool (our industry checks suggest AI music accounts for **0.1% of the royalty pool**)" ([Goldman Sachs Research](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-research/music-in-the-air--focus-on-monetisation--emerging-markets-and-ai)).

## Contested / two-sided

**1. Is AI actually diluting the royalty pool?**
*Claim:* Yes — Deezer and CISAC describe a flood: half of daily uploads, 13.4m tagged tracks in a year, 24% of creator revenue at risk by 2028. Deezer explicitly names "payment dilution" as a two-year fight.
*Counter:* Goldman Sachs measures AI music at **0.1% of the label royalty pool**, with no material revenue impact yet; Deezer's own data says AI is 1–3% of streams and that fraudulent streams are stripped before payout, so the dilution never reaches human artists. Spotify's 1,000-stream monetisation threshold demonetises most low-volume uploads before they touch the pool at all.
*The honest synthesis:* uploads are exploding, monetised listening is not — yet. The threat is optionality (a distributor or platform could start recommending AI at scale) more than current arithmetic.

**2. Did the label AI settlements sell out the musicians who made the recordings?**
*Claim:* The AFM says UMG and WMG monetised session players' recordings as AI training data with no consent, credit or payment, and won't even say which recordings were used.
*Counter:* The majors say the CBA's "new use" clause is a rate-conversion mechanism with no applicable AI rate to convert to, that the union is litigating to gain bargaining leverage, and that opt-in artist participation and revenue sharing are being built into the Udio/Suno platforms.
*Sharpest version of the tension:* the settlements were negotiated by the copyright owners, not the performers — and in most session work the performer never owned anything.

**3. Detection or disclosure — who should be responsible for labelling?**
*Claim (Deezer):* Platforms should detect. It built a classifier, claims 99.8% accuracy on Suno/Udio-class output, tags tracks, and now sells the tech.
*Counter (Spotify, Apple):* Detection creates false positives that punish legitimate hybrid workflows; disclosure via DDEX allows nuance ("AI mastering" ≠ "AI vocals"). Apple's spec literally says "if omitted, none is assumed."
*The obvious hole:* fraudsters will not self-declare. A disclosure regime polices honest artists and misses the bad actors it was built for.

**4. Are AI acts really "charting"?**
*Claim:* Breaking Rust hit No. 1 on a Billboard country chart and Xania Monet reached a Billboard radio airplay chart — proof AI acts can win in the open market.
*Counter:* Breaking Rust needed ~2,500 downloads on a sales-only chart that excludes streaming and radio; TIME ran a piece titled "No, That AI-Generated Country Song Isn't a No. 1 Hit"; terrestrial country radio has not added it. Xania Monet, meanwhile, has human-written lyrics — is that even an "AI artist"?
*Update — the industry answered on 29–30 July 2026, and the argument moved.* Eleven companies proposed, and IFPI adopted worldwide, six eligibility tests: authorised AI service, **"substantially human made,"** no manipulation, rights compliance, no breach of AI-service terms, and consumer-facing AI disclosure ([IFPI, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-rolls-out-global-principles-for-the-eligibility-of-recordings-developed-using-ai-in-official-music-charts-worldwide/)). Applied to the two named acts on an IFPI-network chart, the tests split them: **Xania Monet** — human-written lyrics, Suno vocal, a licensed major-adjacent deal — has a real case for "substantially human made"; **Breaking Rust** — no registered writer at BMI or ASCAP, on a sales-only chart, with the manipulation test aimed squarely at 2,500-download runs — does not obviously survive.
*The new counter:* "Substantially human made" is a standard with no published measurement, no auditor and no appeals process, written by the incumbents whose catalogue it protects — and IFPI's own charts are not the charts these acts actually hit. **[UNVERIFIED]** that Billboard, Luminate or the Official Charts Company have adopted it. So this can be read either as the industry finally drawing a defensible line, or as a cartel of eleven companies deciding who is allowed to be popular.

**5. Whose market forecast do you believe — CISAC's €16bn a year by 2028, or Goldman's $2.1bn by 2030?**
*Claim (CISAC/PMP):* The gen-AI music+AV output market goes from ~€3bn to €64bn by 2028, with €16bn a year in music outputs.
*Counter (Goldman):* Direct AI music revenue reaches ~$2.1bn by 2030. That is an order-of-magnitude disagreement.
*Why:* scope. CISAC counts the value of AI-generated *outputs* wherever they land; Goldman counts revenue accruing to AI music companies. Commercial market-research 2030 estimates range roughly $2.8bn–$12.9bn depending on whether recommendation and mastering engines are folded in — and several firms disagree about the *present* market by 8x, which is the tell that these are not independent measurements. Treat any single point estimate as advocacy.

**6. Should platforms delete AI tracks that nobody streams?**
*Claim (Deezer, Jul 2026):* Yes — remove AI tracks suspected in fraud and AI tracks unstreamed for six months. Storage and catalogue hygiene serve listeners and artists.
*Counter:* A six-month inactivity rule applied only to AI-detected tracks is a content policy dressed as housekeeping — a false positive erases a real person's release, and the appeal process is undefined. Nobody proposes deleting unstreamed *human* tracks (the vast majority of Spotify's catalogue by any measure).

**7. Sony's holdout: principle or leverage?**
*Claim:* Sony is the only major refusing to settle, arguing licensing must precede product. A court ruling would set industry-wide precedent instead of a sealed private deal.
*Counter:* Warner and UMG got paid, got equity-like upside in new products, and got opt-in frameworks two years earlier; Sony's US merits ruling has slipped to 2027 at the earliest, by which point the licensed-model market may already be built without it. Note also that Suno's German loss to GEMA shows litigation *can* win.

## Panel-ready questions

1. Deezer says over half its daily uploads are fully AI-generated but that AI is only 1–3% of streams, and Goldman Sachs puts AI at 0.1% of the label royalty pool. So is "AI is stealing musicians' royalties" simply not true yet — and if it isn't, what exactly are we regulating?
2. Universal settled with Udio, Warner settled with Suno, and neither disclosed a single number. If the terms that will define how AI pays for music are sealed by litigation strategy, should performers and songwriters accept deals they are legally barred from seeing?
3. The AFM says session musicians were licensed into AI training with no consent, credit or payment. Warner's answer is that no AFM rate for AI training exists. Who should bear the cost of that gap — the labels, the AI companies, or the players who already sold their performance for a flat fee?
4. Deezer detects and demonetises; Spotify and Apple ask rightsholders to tick a box, and Apple's spec says an untagged track is assumed non-AI. Which regime is honest, and does voluntary disclosure only ever catch the people who weren't the problem?
5. Since 30 July 2026 a record charts on IFPI-network charts only if it is "substantially human made" — a phrase with no number attached, written by eleven companies including all three majors and HYBE, then applied first to charts in MENA, Southeast Asia, South Africa and Latin America. Who decides what "substantially" means, would Xania Monet's human-written lyrics over a Suno voice pass, and is this global standard-setting or the biggest rightsholders exporting their commercial interest into markets that had no seat at the table?
6. IFPI's 2025 sync revenue fell 2% to $641 million while every other line grew, and CISAC projects gen-AI could be 60% of music library revenue by 2028. If production and library music is the first segment to go, is that a tragedy for working composers — or just the same automation that took commercial illustrators, arriving on schedule?
