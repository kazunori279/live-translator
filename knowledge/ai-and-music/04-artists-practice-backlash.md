# Artists, practice and backlash

**TL;DR for the assistant:** Musicians are not split into "pro-AI" and "anti-AI" camps — most of them are both, and the fight is about where the line sits. Surveys consistently show 70–87% of working musicians already use AI for stem separation, mastering and practice, while 82% say they don't want it generating their music. The organised resistance is very specific: in February 2025 more than 1,000 UK artists including Kate Bush, Annie Lennox, Damon Albarn and Hans Zimmer released a silent album called *Is This What We Want?*, and in April 2024 over 200 artists including Billie Eilish, Nicki Minaj and Stevie Wonder signed an Artist Rights Alliance letter demanding AI companies stop building tools that replace human artistry. Meanwhile the experimental wing — Holly Herndon, Mat Dryhurst, Imogen Heap, Grimes — is trying to build consent-based alternatives, licensing their own voices and turning training-data collection into an art form. As of mid-2026 the sharpest conflict is no longer artists versus AI companies but artists versus their own labels: the American Federation of Musicians is suing Universal and Warner over the Suno and Udio settlements, and 31 artist organisations signed a June 2026 letter demanding "no default opt-ins, no forced AI clauses."

## Key facts

### The exploratory wing

- **Holly Herndon** trained an AI "baby" called **Spawn** on her own and her ensemble's voices for the 2019 album *PROTO*, then released **Holly+** in 2021 — a public voice model of herself governed by a DAO, used for a machine-learning cover of Dolly Parton's "Jolene." She and Mat Dryhurst coined the term **"vocal sovereignty."** ([Whitney Museum artist page](https://whitney.org/artists/21320))
- Herndon and Dryhurst co-founded **Spawning** in 2022 with Jordan Meyer and Patrick Hoepner — the group behind Have I Been Trained? and the **Do Not Train** opt-out registry. ([Whitney](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/xhairymutantx))
- **xhairymutantx** (2024 Whitney Biennial, Mar 20–Aug 11 2024, gallery + artport): they deliberately poisoned their own likeness, training a text-to-image model on grotesquely exaggerated portraits of Herndon so that outputs stored on the Whitney's high-authority domain would leak back into future training sets. The framing question: *"Do we get to choose how we are represented on the AI substrate?"* ([Whitney exhibition page](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/xhairymutantx))
- **The Call** (Serpentine North, Oct 4 2024 – Feb 2 2025): the duo recorded **15 community choirs across the UK** singing a purpose-written songbook of Sacred Harp–derived hymns, then built choral AI models from it under a bespoke **data trust** so choristers retained agency over reuse. Their line, verbatim: *"If all media is training data, including art, let's turn the production of training data into art instead."* ([Serpentine Galleries](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/holly-herndon-mat-dryhurst-the-call/))
- **Grimes**, 23–24 April 2023, on Twitter: *"I'll split 50% royalties on any successful AI generated song that uses my voice… Feel free to use my voice without penalty. I have no label and no legal bindings."* She added: *"I think it's cool to be fused w a machine and I like the idea of open sourcing all art and killing copyright."* ([Consequence, May 2023](https://consequence.net/2023/05/grimes-ai-generated-song-voice/))
- The tool that resulted, **elf.tech**, launched in beta 30 April 2023, built with CreateSafe on its TRINITI engine, with TuneCore distributing approved GrimesAI collaborations on a 50/50 master-royalty split. ([Billboard, 2023](https://www.billboard.com/business/tech/ai-grimes-songs-distributed-tunecore-1235351841/))
- **YACHT** released *Chain Tripping* on DFA Records, 30 August 2019 — nominated for a 2020 Grammy (Best Immersive Audio Album). They fed their own 82-song back catalogue through Char-RNN (lyrics), MusicVAE (MIDI), SampleRNN (raw audio) and NSynth (instrumentation), then hand-assembled "meaningful fragments of plausible nonsense" under a self-imposed rule that they could not add anything the models hadn't produced. Claire L. Evans: *"We didn't set out to produce algorithmically-generated music that could 'pass' as human. We set out to make something meaningful."* ([YACHT on Bandcamp](https://yacht.bandcamp.com/album/chain-tripping))
- **Imogen Heap** built **ai.Mogen** herself with an audio engineer on an open-source model after turning down AI startups, saying she felt she "had no control over the whole thing." First public use: an April 2024 remix of Karin Ann's "false gold." She partnered with the AI platform **Jen** at Web Summit Lisbon on 14 November 2024 and launched five paid "stylefilters" on 24 April 2025 at $4.99–$7.99. ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/music/imogen-heap-aimogen-ai-voice-model/))
- Heap's 2025 13-minute piece **"I AM ___"** credits Jen, Voiceswap and Stability AI as ai.Mogen voice models and over 60 human collaborators. Her framing, verbatim: *"Like many of you, I have major concerns over the unethical training and environmental impacts of AI but the technology itself is not to blame,"* and she wants to *"nurture this 'intelligence' not just as a tool for our amusement but as if we were raising a child."* ([imogenheap.com/iam](https://imogenheap.com/iam))
- **Arca** has used the **Bronze** generative engine since 2019 (a two-year MoMA lobby installation with Philippe Parreno), and in December 2020 released *Riquiquí; Bronze Instances (1-100)* on XL — 100 machine-generated versions of one track. In 2024 she used the real-time **Notochord** MIDI model for a seven-hour magnetic-resonator-piano installation, *The Light Comes in the Name of the Voice*. ([AIMC 2024 paper](https://aimc2024.pubpub.org/pub/0lh6s86c))
- **Brian Eno** — the originator of generative music as a practice — has turned sceptical of generative *AI*. In January 2025 with Stephen Fry he argued you have to *"close off all the avenues towards mediocrity,"* and around his book *What Art Does* he described a "chasm of mediocrity" between algorithmic output and human work, endorsing the test that AI becomes interesting only when it can make you cry. He also tried a song generator on his own style and said nothing it produced was good enough to release. ([Synthtopia, Jan 2025](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2025/01/07/brian-eno-on-artificial-intelligence-you-have-to-close-off-all-the-avenues-towards-mediocrity/))
- **David Guetta**, 3 February 2023, posted a clubfloor stunt he called **"Emin-AI-em"**: ChatGPT wrote two bars in Eminem's style, a separate voice-clone site sang them. He never released it commercially and framed it as a conversation-starter. At the 2023 BRITs: *"I'm sure the future of music is in AI. For sure… but as a tool. Nothing is going to replace taste."* ([Variety, Feb 2023](https://variety.com/2023/music/news/david-guetta-eminem-artificial-intelligence-1235516924/))
- **Timbaland** announced the AI entertainment company **Stage Zero** and its AI "artist" **TaTa** on 5 June 2025, describing her as *"a living, learning, autonomous music artist built with AI"* and pitching a genre called "A-pop." TaTa is built on Suno's **Personas** feature, which locks a voice/sound profile from one generation and reuses it. Timbaland is also a strategic advisor to Suno. ([NPR, 17 June 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/06/17/nx-s1-5431544/timbaland-ai-music-suno-stage-zero-tata-generative))
- The backlash hit fast. In June 2025 Timbaland uploaded producer **K Fresh's** track into Suno and posted an unauthorised AI remix; after a cease-and-desist he apologised publicly: *"I should have done my due diligence before using it… I take full responsibility for that oversight."* Engineer **Young Guru** told him his voice was "too important to do anything like this." TaTa's debut single "Glitch x Pulse" landed in October 2025. ([Stereogum, 21 June 2025](https://stereogum.com/2312616/timbaland-apologizes-to-producer-amid-ai-record-label-controversy/news))
- **Taryn Southern** released *I AM AI* in 2018, generally cited as the first album composed and produced with commercial AI tools (Amper Music). **[UNVERIFIED — not re-confirmed against a primary source in this research pass; don't quote a specific date.]**

### The organised resistance

- **"Is This What We Want?"** — released 25 February 2025 by more than **1,000 UK artists**, timed to the closing day of the UK government's copyright-and-AI consultation. Twelve tracks of recordings of **empty studios and performance spaces**; the track titles spell out *"The British Government Must Not Legalise Music Theft To Benefit AI Companies."* No individual credits — every track is credited to "1,000 UK Artists." All profits to Help Musicians. It reached **No. 38 on the UK albums chart**; a vinyl edition with a bonus Paul McCartney track went on pre-order in November 2025 at £28. ([isthiswhatwewant.com](https://www.isthiswhatwewant.com/))
- Contributors included Kate Bush, Annie Lennox, Damon Albarn, Hans Zimmer, Max Richter, Yusuf / Cat Stevens, Tori Amos, Imogen Heap, Billy Ocean, Riz Ahmed, Ed O'Brien, The Kanneh-Masons and The King's Singers. **Kate Bush:** *"In the music of the future, will our voices go unheard?"* ([NPR, 28 Feb 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/02/28/nx-s1-5311909/haydn-louis-armstrong-kate-bush-1000-ai-copyright-law-uk-silence-album))
- **Artist Rights Alliance open letter, 2 April 2024**, 200+ signatories. The demand, verbatim: developers and platforms must *"cease the use of artificial intelligence (AI) to infringe upon and devalue the rights of human artists"* and *"pledge that they will not develop or deploy AI music-generation technology, content, or tools that undermine or replace the human artistry of songwriters and artists."* It warns of *"a race to the bottom that will degrade the value of our work."* Signatories include Stevie Wonder, Billie Eilish, Nicki Minaj, R.E.M., Katy Perry, Sheryl Crow, Smokey Robinson, Pearl Jam, Jon Batiste, Elvis Costello, Kacey Musgraves, Chappell Roan, Noah Kahan, and the estates of Bob Marley and Frank Sinatra. ARA was co-founded by Rosanne Cash. ([Artist Rights Alliance](https://artistrightsnow.medium.com/200-artists-call-on-ai-developers-tech-platforms-not-to-devalue-music-and-undermine-artists-2727e17bc10a))
- Notably, ARA executive director Jen Jacobsen said the letter was *not* a legislative push: "We're not thinking about legislation here." It was aimed squarely at tech companies. ([Axios, 2 Apr 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/04/02/musicians-letter-ai-replace-artists))
- **Ed Newton-Rex** resigned as VP of Audio at Stability AI in November 2023 after the company told the US Copyright Office that training on copyrighted work was fair use — his own team had licensed everything for Stable Audio. He founded **Fairly Trained** in January 2024. ([Fairly Trained](https://www.fairlytrained.org/about))
- Fairly Trained currently lists **21 certified entities** — 12 company certifications, 2 product, 7 model — and almost all of them are music: Beatoven.ai, Endel, Infinite Album, Lemonaide, LifeScore, Musical AI, Rightsify, Soundful, Splash, Tuney, Voicemod, Voice-Swap; products Boomy and Frostbite Orckings; models Jen, Kits, KL3M, Mitsua Likes, Vocalist.ai. Note the loop: Imogen Heap's partner Jen is one of them. ([Fairly Trained certified models](https://www.fairlytrained.org/certified-models))
- **Nick Cave**, Red Hand Files #218, January 2023, remains the most quotable rejection: *"With all the love and respect in the world, this song is bullshit, a grotesque mockery of what it is to be human."* Also: *"What ChatGPT is, in this instance, is replication as travesty"*; *"Songs arise out of suffering… algorithms don't feel. Data doesn't suffer"*; *"ChatGPT has no inner being, it has been nowhere, it has endured nothing."* ([The Red Hand Files](https://www.theredhandfiles.com/chat-gpt-what-do-you-think/))
- **Björn Ulvaeus** (ABBA, president of CISAC) is the careful middle: *"There is, of course, no way we can or should stand against AI. We're not Luddites"* — but *"if badly regulated, generative AI also has the power to cause great damage to human creators, to their careers and livelihoods."* ([CISAC, 2 Dec 2024](https://www.cisac.org/Newsroom/news-releases/global-economic-study-shows-human-creators-future-risk-generative-ai))

### Unions and collective bodies

- **AFM v. Warner Music Group Corp. and Universal Music Group, Inc.**, Case No. 1:26-cv-04760, S.D.N.Y., filed **5 June 2026**. The complaint's opening allegation, close to verbatim: the labels *"have licensed sound recordings on which AFM-represented musicians have worked, without compensation or credit, to two AI companies"* and refused to tell the union which recordings and whose work is involved. The AFM invokes Article 21 "new use" provisions of the Sound Recording Labor Agreement. An amended complaint followed on 24 July 2026 adding Warner Records Inc. and Atlantic Recording Corp. ([complaint PDF, via MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/files/2026/06/AFM-vs-Warner-UMG.pdf))
- The complaint's rhetorical hook: it quotes the labels' own June 2024 lawsuits against Suno and Udio back at them — the majors warned that irresponsibly developed AI "threaten[s] enduring and irreparable harm to recording artists," then licensed those same companies. The prior SRLA expired 31 January 2026; successor talks began January 2026 with AI consent/compensation/credit as a centrepiece. Sony Music, which has not settled with either AI company, is not a defendant. ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-musicians-union-files-amended-lawsuit-against-universal-and-warner-over-suno-and-udio-ai-deals/))
- **Council of Music Makers** (Ivors Academy, Featured Artists Coalition, Musicians' Union, Music Producers Guild, Music Managers Forum), November 2025: demanded an industry-wide standard of **"consent, control and compensation,"** with consent being **opt-in, not opt-out**, and dismissed label messaging as "misleading rhetoric" and "deceitful buzzwords." ([CMU](https://completemusicupdate.com/misleading-rhetoric-and-deceitful-buzzwords-not-good-enough-says-council-of-music-makers-in-call-for-creator-consent-control-and-compensation-in-ai-deals/))
- **EMMA-coordinated open letter, 22 June 2026** — **31 organisations** including the Music Artists Coalition, Artist Rights Alliance, Songwriters of North America, ECSA, the Black Music Action Coalition and the Featured Artists Coalition. The grievance is aimed at labels, not AI firms: artists on existing deals are receiving letters saying they will be **opted in by default**, and new contracts carry AI clauses as standard. Demands: *"No default opt-ins," "No forced AI clauses,"* and no use of an artist's work, voice, performance, likeness or creative identity without meaningful consent, fair remuneration and full transparency. Closing line: *"The future of music must be built with artists, songwriters and their representatives, not imposed on them."* ([MBW, 22 June 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/irving-azoffs-music-artists-coalition-among-31-groups-warning-against-misuse-of-artist-and-songwriter-rights-in-ai-deals/))
- **Irving Azoff**, after the UMG–Udio settlement: *"Everyone talks about 'partnership,' but artists end up on the sidelines with scraps."* ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/universal-music-udio-ai-deal-music-industry-reactions/))
- **SAG-AFTRA's Sound Recordings Code**, ratified April 2024 with UMG, Sony, Warner and Disney Music, requires "clear and conspicuous" consent, minimum compensation and disclosure of intended use for synthetic voices — but it **does not require consent for AI training** and sets **no minimum payment** for training use. Session fees rose 14.5% immediately, then 3.75% (Jan 2025), 3.25% (Jan 2026), 3% (Jan 2027). ([SAG-AFTRA AI appendix, PDF](https://www.sagaftra.org/sites/default/files/Sound%20Recordings%20Code%20Appendix%20on%20Artificial%20Intelligence.pdf))
- SAG-AFTRA's **Narrativ** digital voice replica agreement (August 2024) was a genuine consent/compensation/control template — performer approval per ad, performer-set rates above union minimum, deletion on exit. It **ceased to be in effect on 24 September 2025**. ([SAG-AFTRA](https://www.sagaftra.org/sag-aftra-and-narrativ-announce-new-agreement))
- **UK Music's "This Is Music" creator survey, 27 November 2025**: **66%** of creators agree "AI poses a threat to my career as a creator"; only 13% disagree. Over **90%** back protections on consent, payment, labelling of AI content and protection of voice and image. ([UK Music](https://www.ukmusic.org/news/two-thirds-of-creators-say-ai-poses-a-threat-to-their-careers-uk-music-report-reveals/))

### Where the line actually sits in practice

- **Moises × Water & Music survey**, n=1,525 musicians, late 2025. 67% use AI for music work. Among users: **stem separation 71%**, practice/skill development 44%, accompaniment generation 40%, **mixing and mastering 32%**, melody/chord ideas 28%, **full song generation from text 24%**. Professionals out-adopt hobbyists 78% to 60%. **Caveat: ~80% of respondents came from Moises' own user base.** ([Moises / Water & Music](https://moises.ai/insights/musician-ai-report-water-and-music/))
- **Muse Group survey**, n=1,200 users of Ultimate Guitar, MuseScore, MuseHub and Audacity, published 10 April 2026: **78% open to AI tools, 70% already using them — and 82% oppose AI generating music.** Support by task: cleanup 70%, practice assistance 68%, transcription 58%, lyrics generation 37%, auto-finishing compositions 32%. 93% would prefer a slower tool with more oversight. Counter-intuitive finding: **18–24s are the most opposed group (28%)**, not the oldest. ([Muse Group](https://www.mu.se/posts/muse-group-ai-survey))
- **LANDR study**, n=1,241, fielded 30 Sept – 6 Oct 2025: 87% of artists use AI somewhere in their workflow; 29% use song generators, mostly for *parts* (a vocal, an instrument) rather than complete tracks. Same selection-bias caveat — LANDR's own community. ([LANDR](https://www.landr.com/ai))
- The consistent shape across all three, despite different sampling: **the acceptability curve tracks how close the tool gets to authorship.** Cleanup and separation ~70%; mastering ~32%; full generation 18–29%. Voice cloning is rarely broken out as its own line item, which is itself a gap in the data.

### Ghost artists and AI acts on playlists

- **Liz Pelly, "The Ghosts in the Machine,"** Harper's, January 2025 issue (excerpted from *Mood Machine*, published 7 Jan 2025). Reports that Spotify's **"Perfect Fit Content"** programme, introduced to editors in **2017**, sourced cheap production music from a small set of companies: roughly **20 songwriters behind more than 500 "artists,"** with playlists including **Ambient Relaxation, Deep Focus, Cocktail Jazz and Bossa Nova Dinner** almost entirely composed of PFC tracks, and hundreds of playlists monitored by the PFC team by 2023. Spotify called "fake artists" allegations *"categorically untrue, full stop"* — while stopping short of denying it placed such tracks on playlists. **[Harper's is paywalled — the article URL returns a payment wall to automated fetches; use the free summary if you need to check details.]** ([Harper's, paywalled](https://harpers.org/archive/2025/01/the-ghosts-in-the-machine-liz-pelly-spotify-musicians/) · [free summary at CDM](https://cdm.link/liz-pelly-spotify-ghost-content/))
- A neat detail for a panel: the writer David Turner showed Spotify's "Ambient Chill" playlist had been cleared of **Brian Eno**, Bibio and Jon Hopkins and refilled with production-library tracks. The generative-music pioneer got algorithmically replaced by generic ambient before AI generation was even a factor.
- **The Velvet Sundown** hit roughly **1 million monthly Spotify listeners** in June 2025 with two albums of fake-1970s folk rock and four fictional band members. Deezer's detector said **100% of the tracks were AI**; Music Ally traced most playlist placements to just four Spotify accounts. On 5 July 2025 the project admitted it is *"a synthetic music project guided by human creative direction, and composed, voiced, and visualized with the support of artificial intelligence."* A pseudonymous "Andrew Frelon" had meanwhile hoaxed Rolling Stone and the Washington Post by posing as the band's spokesperson. ([The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2025/07/07/the-velvet-sundown-ai-band-story-explained))
- **Breaking Rust**, an AI country act with no human performers, took **No. 1 on Billboard's Country Digital Song Sales chart** with "Walk My Walk" in November 2025 — the first AI-created song to top a Billboard chart. Crucial caveat for the panel: that chart counts **paid downloads only**, and Luminate data implies roughly **2,500 downloads** (about $3,000 of sales) was enough. Country radio did not add it. ([Axios, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/14/ai-country-song-artist-breaking-rust-tops-chart))
- **Xania Monet** is the hardest case, because there *is* a human author. Mississippi poet **Telisha "Nikki" Jones** writes the lyrics and generates the music with Suno. Bidding reached **$3 million**; **Hallwood Media** (ex-Interscope's Neil Jacobson) signed her in September 2025. First Billboard entry week of 20 Sept 2025 (No. 25 Emerging Artists, No. 21 Hot Gospel Songs); "How Was I Supposed to Know" became the **first AI song on a Billboard radio airplay chart**, entering Adult R&B Airplay at No. 30 dated 11 Nov 2025. Kehlani criticised the deal, saying Jones was "doing none of the work." ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/ai-music-artist-xania-monet-multimillion-dollar-record-deal/) · [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xania_Monet))
- **Spotify's 25 September 2025 AI policy** did three things: an impersonation rule requiring the impersonated artist's authorisation for any vocal replica; a spam filter (Spotify says it removed **75 million+ spammy tracks** in the preceding 12 months); and support for a **DDEX** industry standard for AI disclosure in credits, with 15 launch distributors — notably **not** the three majors at announcement. Spotify explicitly said disclosure would **not** be used to downrank tracks. ([Spotify Newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/))

## Contested / two-sided

**1. Is licensing your own voice empowerment, or does it normalise the thing artists are fighting?**
Grimes and Imogen Heap argue consent plus a revenue share turns an unavoidable technology into an artist-controlled asset — Heap has built lineage tracking so uses show up as collaborations, and Grimes offered a flat 50%. The counter, made explicitly by the Council of Music Makers and implicitly by Nick Cave: once a superstar with leverage licenses her voice, the practice becomes a market norm, and the session singer with no leverage gets offered a buyout instead of a royalty. Note Grimes' own later position is nuanced — she has criticised current AI music apps as "slop oriented" while defending consent-based voice use.

**2. Is the flood of AI uploads an economic emergency, or a statistical illusion?**
Deezer's own numbers cut both ways: **over 50% of new uploads** are fully AI-generated, but AI tracks take only **1–3% of streams**, and up to **85%** of those streams were fraudulent. So the alarmist read ("music is being drowned") and the dismissive read ("nobody is listening to it") are supported by the same dataset. The bridge argument, made by CISAC, is that dilution of the royalty pool and displacement of production-music/library work happens long before consumers notice.

**3. Did the Suno/Udio settlements protect artists or sell them?**
Warner CEO Robert Kyncl framed the deals as pro-artist because they are **opt-in** for name, image, likeness, voice and compositions. Against that: the AFM says thousands of session musicians' performances were licensed with **no compensation, no credit and no notice** (Case 1:26-cv-04760); the EMMA letter says artists are being **opted in by default** in practice; independent artists have no opt-in mechanism at all; and no revenue splits have been disclosed. Warner has told the court the session-musician pay clause simply "cannot function" for AI use.

**4. Is the mastering/stems-fine, generation-not line a principle or a convenience?**
The survey data shows a sharp gradient, and most musicians treat it as obvious. But Holly Herndon's position undercuts it: *all* media is training data, so the stem separator you like was also trained on somebody's recordings without asking. If the objection is unlicensed training, mastering AI is not innocent; if the objection is replacement of human labour, stem separation already replaced a job that remix engineers used to do.

**5. Does labelling help artists or create a stigma tier?**
80% of listeners want AI music labelled and 69% want it paid less. Academic work suggests listeners **engage less deeply with music labelled AI even when a human made it**, which is exactly what disclosure advocates want and exactly what Spotify says it will not enforce ("won't force tracks into a false binary"). The evidence is not unanimous — at least one 2025 study found AI-labelled pop rated *higher* on positive emotion — so the honest framing is that the direction of the labelling effect is genuinely unsettled. ([MBW on the labelling study](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/listeners-engage-less-deeply-with-music-labeled-as-ai-even-when-its-actually-human-made-academic-study-finds/))

**6. Xania Monet: is a poet with a generator an artist?**
Jones writes every lyric; Suno makes every sound. Kehlani says she is "doing none of the work." The counter: songwriters who never sing or play have always been credited as artists, and the US Copyright Office already sanctions AI as an "assistive tool" while refusing protection where a machine determines the expressive elements — which means the answer may be settled by registration practice rather than aesthetics.

**7. Timbaland: producer's instrument, or the vanishing of the singer?**
He says the TaTa video is "85% human-made" and that he has an album coming with all-independent new artists. The critique from Young Guru and from Black music commentators is sharper than "AI bad": a producer with the leverage to launch careers built a synthetic Asian woman instead of signing a real one, using a platform he advises, trained on catalogues including work he did not clear.

## Panel-ready questions

1. **Every survey says musicians happily use AI for stem separation and mastering but reject AI generation. Is that a coherent ethical line, or just the line that happens to protect the jobs in this room?** (Push: the stem separator was trained on unlicensed recordings too.)
2. **The AFM is suing Universal and Warner — not Suno and Udio — over the AI settlements. Has the real conflict moved from artists-versus-tech to artists-versus-their-own-labels, and if so, who is left to negotiate on artists' behalf?**
3. **Grimes offered 50% of royalties to anyone using an AI clone of her voice. Imogen Heap trained her own. Was that leadership, or did they establish a market price that will be used against every session singer with no leverage?**
4. **Over half of new uploads to Deezer are fully AI-generated, but AI tracks take 1–3% of streams and up to 85% of those streams are fraudulent. Which number should scare a working musician more — and does "AI slop" describe a real threat or a moral panic?**
5. **97% of listeners can't tell AI from human music in a blind test, and studies suggest people rate music lower simply because it's labelled AI. If the audience can't hear the difference and only the label changes their judgement, what exactly are we protecting — the music, or the story about who made it?**
