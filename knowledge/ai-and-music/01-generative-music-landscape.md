# The generative music landscape

**TL;DR for the assistant:** As of August 2026 the generative music market has consolidated around one clear leader — Suno, which hit 2 million paid subscribers and $300 million in annual recurring revenue in February 2026 and raised $400 million at a $5.4 billion valuation in June 2026. The defining shift of the last twelve months is not better audio quality but *legitimisation through licensing*: Universal settled with Udio in October 2025, Warner settled with Suno in November 2025, and Klay Vision signed all three majors before it ever shipped a product. Sony and Universal are still suing Suno in Boston, and a German court in Munich ruled against Suno on 31 July 2026 — the first European decision holding that a music model "memorised" copyrighted works. Meanwhile the product frontier has moved from text-to-music prompts toward stems, inpainting, real-time steering and DAW integration: Suno Studio, ElevenLabs Music v2, Google's Lyria RealTime. And the volume is staggering — Deezer says more than half of all new tracks uploaded to it daily are now fully AI-generated, about 90,000 songs a day.

## Key facts

### Suno (Cambridge, MA — the market leader)

- Suno raised **$400 million at a $5.4 billion post-money valuation** in a Series D led by Bond Capital, announced **3 June 2026** — more than doubling its $2.45 billion valuation from a $250 million Series C in November 2025 ([Music Ally, 4 Jun 2026](https://musically.com/2026/06/04/suno-raises-400m-funding-and-teases-its-first-licensed-model/)). Earlier rounds: $125m Series B in May 2024 at roughly $500m.
- CEO Mikey Shulman announced **2 million paid subscribers and $300m ARR on 25 February 2026**, up from ~1 million subscribers and $200m ARR in November 2025 — a doubling of subscribers in three months ([TechCrunch, 27 Feb 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/ai-music-generator-suno-hits-2-million-paid-subscribers-and-300m-in-annual-recurring-revenue/)).
- **Pricing:** Free tier (50 daily non-cumulative credits, no commercial use); Pro at $8/month annual (~$10 monthly) for 2,500 credits; Premier at $24/month annual (~$30 monthly) for 10,000 credits plus Suno Studio ([suno.com/pricing](https://suno.com/pricing)).
- **Output rights are tier-dependent.** Suno's terms assign paid users "all of its right, title and interest in and to any Output," but explicitly warn it "makes no representation or warranty to you that any copyright will vest in any Output." Free users get personal, non-commercial use only, with attribution required ([Suno Terms of Service](https://suno.com/terms)).
- **Warner Music settled and partnered on 25 November 2025** — the first major-label deal for Suno. Terms: Suno launches new *licensed* models in 2026 and deprecates current models; downloads become paid-only with monthly caps; Warner artists get opt-in control over name, image, likeness, voice and compositions; and Suno acquired concert-discovery platform **Songkick** from Warner ([Music Business Worldwide, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-settles-with-suno-strikes-first-of-its-kind-deal-with-ai-song-generator/)).
- **Product direction:** v5 and Suno Studio shipped September 2025; v5.5 added Voices, Custom Models and "My Taste". Studio is a browser-based "generative audio workstation" with up to 12 time-aligned generative stems exported as WAV or MIDI — separation derived from the model's internal representation rather than frequency-domain source separation. [UNVERIFIED — stem count and internals come from third-party guides, not Suno documentation.]

### Udio (New York — the cautionary tale)

- **Universal Music Group settled with Udio on 29 October 2025** and announced a joint "new licensed AI music creation platform" launching in 2026, trained on "authorized and licensed music," with UMG receiving a compensatory legal settlement plus recording and publishing licences ([UMG/Udio press release, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/universal-music-group-and-udio-announce-udios-first-strategic-agreements-for-new-licensed-ai-music-creation-platform-302599129.html)).
- The settlement created a **walled garden**: Udio disabled downloads, video export and stem export overnight, including for paying subscribers with pre-existing tracks. After a user revolt Udio reopened downloads for a 48-hour window starting 3 November 2025.
- **Downloads have not been restored as of mid-2026** and Udio's own changelog shows almost nothing shipped in 2026 — one dated header (28 January 2026) with no listed features; the most recent substantive entries are Voices (11 Sep 2025) and Sessions (26 Jun 2025) ([Udio changelog](https://help.udio.com/en/articles/10748731-changelog-what-s-new-with-udio)). This is the single strongest signal that the licensed relaunch has slipped.
- Warner settled with Udio in November 2025; Merlin (Dec 2025) and Kobalt (Jan 2026) followed. **Sony Music remains in litigation.** In June 2026 a New York judge refused Sony's motion to add 30,442 sound recordings, keeping the Udio case at 333 works.

### Google / DeepMind (Lyria family)

- **Lyria 3 launched 18 February 2026** in beta in the Gemini app: 30-second tracks from a text description *or an uploaded image*, with auto-generated lyrics and custom cover art, in eight languages. Also surfaced on YouTube's Dream Track ([Google blog, 18 Feb 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/lyria-3/)).
- **Lyria 3 Pro launched 25 March 2026** — tracks up to 3 minutes with structural control over intros, verses, choruses and bridges. Available in Vertex AI (public preview), Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, Google Vids, the Gemini app, and ProducerAI ([Google blog, 25 Mar 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/lyria-3-pro/)).
- **Lyria 3.5** is now the model DeepMind lists as latest — "improved vocals, musicality, and variable song lengths," up to 3 minutes ([DeepMind Lyria page](https://deepmind.google/models/lyria/)). No public launch date is given on that page.
- **Lyria RealTime** (`models/lyria-realtime-exp`) is the most architecturally distinct product in the field: a bidirectional WebSocket stream producing 48 kHz stereo 16-bit PCM that you steer live. Controls include BPM (60–200), density (0–1), brightness (0–1), scale (12 keys), guidance (0–6), and mute_bass / mute_drums / only_bass_and_drums toggles. **Instrumental only — no vocals** ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/realtime-music-generation)).
- All Lyria outputs carry **SynthID** imperceptible watermarking, and Google says it runs filters checking outputs against existing content and designed the model "for original expression, not for mimicking existing artists."
- DeepMind also maintains **Magenta RealTime**, an open model for real-time generation.

### ElevenLabs (Eleven Music)

- **Eleven Music launched 5 August 2025** with licensing deals signed *before* launch with **Merlin** (indie label consortium — Adele, Nirvana, Mitski, Phoebe Bridgers) and **Kobalt** (Beck, Bon Iver, Childish Gambino), both strictly opt-in with revenue sharing ([TechCrunch, 5 Aug 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/05/elevenlabs-launches-an-ai-music-generator-which-it-claims-is-cleared-for-commercial-use/)).
- **Music v2 shipped 26 May 2026** with the field's clearest move toward editing over generation: inpainting to regenerate any selected section, section-by-section composition (intro/verse/chorus), and mid-track genre transitions. ElevenLabs cut prices **up to 50% for API customers and up to 40% for self-serve Creative users**, and named a new licensing collaboration with **Believe** ([ElevenLabs blog, 26 May 2026](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/introducing-music-v2)).
- ElevenLabs states the model is "trained only on licensed data and cleared for commercial use," with no sync fees or clearance delays. Film, TV and large studio game rights require an Enterprise plan. No UMG/Sony/Warner deal has been announced.

### Klay Vision (license-first challenger)

- On **20 November 2025 Klay Vision became the first AI music company to sign all three majors simultaneously** — UMG, Sony and Warner, plus their publishing arms — with a "large music model" trained exclusively on licensed material ([Al Jazeera, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/11/20/sony-warner-and-universal-sign-ai-music-licensing-deals-with-startup-klay)). Warner announced separate deals with Udio and **Stability AI** the same week.
- Klay is led by CEO Ary Attie with Thomas Hesse (ex-Sony), Björn Winckler (ex-DeepMind music team) and Brian Whitman (Echo Nest / Spotify). The pitch is "active listening" — a subscription streaming service where users remake existing songs in different styles rather than generate from scratch. [UNVERIFIED — mid-2026 launch timing and Spotify integration come from secondary coverage only.]
- On **10 June 2026** the NMPA announced industry-wide template AI licensing deals with both Udio and Klay, letting independent US publishers opt in, with AI licensing income split **50/50 between compositions and recordings** — a sharp break from streaming, where recordings take roughly 3x what songs do.

### Stability AI

- **Stable Audio 2.5** is positioned as the first enterprise-targeted audio model: sub-2-second inference for tracks up to 3 minutes, reduced from 50 diffusion steps to 8 via the Adversarial Relativistic-Contrastive (ARC) post-training method, plus audio inpainting. Stability says it is "commercially safe and trained on a fully licensed dataset." Distributed via the Stability API, fal, Replicate, ComfyUI and on-prem, with a co-development partnership with sound-branding agency **amp** (Landor Group / WPP) and availability through WPP Open ([Stability AI](https://stability.ai/news-updates/stability-ai-introduces-stable-audio-25-the-first-audio-model-built-for-enterprise-sound-production-at-scale)). Launch date reported as September 2025; see Open questions.
- Strategic read: Stability has effectively conceded consumer text-to-music to Suno and is selling brand sonic-identity fine-tunes to agencies instead.

### Open weights and China

- **ACE-Step 1.5** (ACE Studio + StepFun, arXiv 31 Jan 2026) is the strongest open-weights music model: full songs in **under 2 seconds on an A100, under 10 seconds on an RTX 3090, under 4 GB VRAM**, a hybrid LM-planner plus Diffusion Transformer, 50+ language lyric alignment, LoRA personalisation, and intrinsic RL with no external reward model ([arXiv 2602.00744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00744)). Code and weights are public.
- **Kunlun Tech's Mureka** (China) reached V8 on 28 January 2026 and claims "publishable" quality; subscriptions run ¥88/month for up to 180 tracks with commercial rights. Annualised revenue reportedly hit ~$12m by November 2025 — roughly 25x smaller than Suno — with over 90% of B2B clients overseas. **MiniMax Music 2.5/2.6** competes on price at roughly $0.035–$0.075 per generation on third-party inference platforms. ByteDance (Seed Music / Sponge Music), Tencent (LeVo 2, non-commercial licence) and NetEase all maintain positions without pursuing direct profit. [UNVERIFIED — Chinese revenue and pricing figures come from aggregator coverage, not company filings.]
- **Meta's AudioCraft / MusicGen is dormant, not dead.** MusicGen was trained on ~20,000 hours (about 400,000 recordings) Meta owned or licensed. Code is MIT-licensed but **weights are CC-BY-NC 4.0 — non-commercial only**. The repo still pins Python 3.9 and PyTorch 2.1.0, a 2023 stack, and Meta has shipped no successor flagship music model ([Meta AudioCraft](https://ai.meta.com/blog/audiocraft-musicgen-audiogen-encodec-generative-ai-audio/)).

### The tooling layer: consolidation, not shutdowns

- **Splice acquired Kits AI on 21 January 2026** — 7m+ users, 80m+ minutes of vocals processed, and the first platform to offer Licensed Artist Voice Models; it is certified **Fairly Trained**. This followed Splice's ~$50m acquisition of Spitfire Audio in May 2025 and a December 2025 partnership with UMG ([MBW, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/splice-acquires-ai-powered-voice-production-platform-kits-ai/)).
- **LANDR acquired Reason Studios** (announced early January 2026), bolting a legacy DAW onto an AI mastering and distribution business; LANDR also owns Synchro Arts (VocAlign, Revoice Pro).
- **Moises / Music.AI** raised a $40m Series A (reported January 2025) and stayed in stem separation and practice tools rather than generation; Fender's Studio Pro DAW integrates Moises stem separation.
- **Boomy and Mubert are both still operating** and neither has shut down. Mubert's differentiator remains continuous generative streaming rather than discrete track generation, from roughly $14/month. Boomy's reputational baggage dates to May 2023, when Spotify removed roughly 7% of Boomy-created songs over alleged bot-boosted streaming.
- **Riffusion** launched its Fuzz model in public beta on 31 January 2025 and made it free worldwide; the company remains small (roughly 15 employees, Menlo Park) with $4m raised in a 2023 Greycroft-led seed round backed by The Chainsmokers. It has not kept pace with Suno.
- **OpenAI** has reportedly been building a music generation tool since at least October 2025 — text and audio prompts to full compositions, accompaniment for vocal tracks, music for video — with Juilliard students annotating scores rather than training solely on published recordings. **Not announced, not shipped.** [UNVERIFIED — sourced to The Information via TechCrunch; OpenAI has never confirmed it.]

### Distribution-side reality check

- **Deezer, 21 July 2026:** fully AI-generated recordings exceeded **50% of daily uploads** at peak in June 2026 — about **90,000 tracks a day**. But AI tracks account for only **1–3% of total streams**, and Deezer says **85% of streams on AI tracks are fraudulent** and are demonetised. Deezer detected and tagged 13.4 million AI tracks in 2025 alone, claiming 99.8% accuracy ([Deezer newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/music-streamer-deezer-says-more-than-50-of-daily-uploads-are-ai-generated/)).
- Deezer's detector only flags **100% AI-generated** recordings from prolific models like Suno and Udio — it does not catch partially AI-assisted songs, so the true "AI-touched" share is higher and unmeasured.
- **Spotify, 25 September 2025:** three-pillar policy — an impersonation policy for unauthorised voice clones, a music spam filter, and DDEX-standard AI disclosure credits with 18 partners including Amuse, Believe, DistroKid, EMPIRE and FUGA. Spotify said it removed **over 75 million spammy tracks in the preceding 12 months** and stressed the policy is "not about punishing or downranking responsible AI use" ([Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/)). Spotify launched AI Credits in beta in April 2026; Apple Music launched Transparency Tags in March 2026.

### Litigation status (the variable everything else depends on)

- **UMG & Sony v. Suno (D. Mass.)** is the live US bellwether. Suno conceded use of copyrighted recordings early, leaving fair use as the only defence. A 30 June 2026 amended schedule runs fact discovery to **30 September 2026** and dispositive motions to **9 April 2027** — so the widely trailed "summer 2026 fair use ruling" is not happening; a merits decision is a 2027 event at the earliest ([MBW, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-a-fight-over-61000-recordings-could-shape-the-future-of-ai-music-licensing/)).
- The labels are fighting to expand the case from 560 works to **61,026 recordings**, identified by audio fingerprinting. At the $150,000 statutory ceiling for willful infringement that is **over $9 billion** in theoretical exposure versus roughly $84 million on the original complaint.
- **GEMA v. Suno, Munich I Regional Court, 31 July 2026:** Judge Elke Schwager ruled for GEMA on essentially all points over six songs including "Rasputin," "Daddy Cool" and "Mambo No. 5." The court found the works were "reproducibly contained" in Suno's v3.5 and v4 models on German servers, ruled out randomness as an explanation, rejected both the EU text-and-data-mining exception and US fair use, and found Suno used stream-ripping that circumvented YouTube's rolling cipher. Remedies: injunction, disclosure and damages, amount TBD; not yet enforceable. Suno says the ruling "rests on a fundamental mischaracterization" and is weighing appeal ([Music Ally, 31 Jul 2026](https://musically.com/2026/07/31/german-collecting-society-gema-wins-its-copyright-infringement-lawsuit-against-suno/)).

## Contested / two-sided

**1. Did the settlements vindicate artists or just price the theft?**
Warner CEO Robert Kyncl called the Suno deal "a victory for the creative community that benefits everyone," and MBW argued the UMG–Udio settlement was "a win for musicians everywhere." The counter, put most sharply by Virginie Berger in Forbes (18 Dec 2025), is "launch, train, settle" — the deals retroactively monetised infringement that already happened, at a price the infringer could comfortably pay, and set a template where breaking the rules first is the cheapest route to a licence. The **American Federation of Musicians filed an amended lawsuit against Universal and Warner** alleging they licensed members' recordings to Suno and Udio "without compensation or credit" and failed to share settlement proceeds; both majors are seeking dismissal. On 24 February 2026 the Music Artist Coalition, ECSA and Artist Rights Institute published a "Say No to Suno" open letter.

**2. Is training on copyrighted recordings fair use?**
Suno and Udio's position: training is transformative statistical learning, not copying; outputs are new works; the EU TDM exception and US fair use both cover it. The Munich court rejected exactly this on 31 July 2026, finding the works were "reproducibly contained" in the model weights — a *memorisation* theory that, if adopted in the US, converts the training question into a reproduction question and largely ends the fair use defence. Suno counters that the ruling "rests on a fundamental mischaracterization of how Suno's technology works." Note the asymmetry: the German ruling is not binding US precedent, and the US court has not ruled at all.

**3. Does "licensed training data" actually mean anything?**
ElevenLabs, Stability, Klay and Kits AI all market clean provenance. Skeptics point out that (a) "licensed" claims are largely unaudited — Fairly Trained certification is one of very few third-party checks and only a handful of companies hold it; (b) opt-in regimes at Merlin and Kobalt mean the *dataset* may be far narrower than the marketing implies; and (c) Stability made a "fully licensed dataset" claim while facing multiple copyright suits over its other models. The counter-counter: Klay signed all three majors *before* launching, which is a materially different posture from settling after the fact.

**4. Walled garden vs. ownership.**
Udio's UMG deal killed downloads — you can create and share inside the app but not export. UMG's logic is containment: no exports means no flood of AI tracks into DSPs and no untracked derivative works. Users' logic is that they paid for a creation tool and got a toy; the backlash was severe enough to force a 48-hour download amnesty in November 2025, and Udio has shipped essentially nothing since. Suno took the opposite path — keep downloads, gate them behind paid tiers with monthly caps. Both are bets on what "owning" an AI-generated song should mean.

**5. Is >50% AI uploads a crisis or a non-event?**
Crisis reading: more than half of new music delivered to Deezer is machine-made, royalty pools are diluted, and 85% of AI streams are fraudulent — this is an industrial-scale attack on the payout system. Non-event reading: AI tracks take only **1–3% of actual listening**, precisely because Deezer excludes them from algorithmic and editorial playlists. Supply is exploding; demand is not. The interesting question is whether that gap holds once licensed, artist-endorsed AI music (Udio/UMG, Klay) enters the *recommended* pool rather than the spam pool.

**6. Does the field even need frontier labs?**
ACE-Step 1.5 generates a full song in under 2 seconds on an A100 and runs in under 4 GB of VRAM, with open weights. If open models are within striking distance of commercial quality, the moat for Suno is not the model — it is licences, distribution and brand. Counter-argument: Suno's $300m ARR against Mureka's ~$12m suggests consumers pay for product polish and catalogue legitimacy, not raw model quality, and open weights compete on neither.

## Panel-ready questions

1. Warner and Universal settled with the companies they said stole from their artists, then took equity-adjacent upside in the outcome. The AFM is now suing them for not passing that money through. **Did the majors sell out their artists, or did they get the best deal available in a fight they might have lost?**
2. A Munich court just ruled that six songs were "reproducibly contained" inside Suno's model weights. **If a model can be shown to memorise, does the entire "learning, not copying" defence collapse — and should the legal test be about the training process or about what can be extracted from the weights?**
3. Udio's licensed platform means you can make music but never take it out of the building. **Is a walled garden where you don't own your output actually a creative tool, or is it karaoke with extra steps — and would you accept those terms for Photoshop?**
4. More than half of everything uploaded to Deezer daily is now fully AI-generated, but AI takes 1–3% of streams. **Is the flood a genuine threat to working musicians' income, or is the real threat something else entirely — the collapse of sync, library and production-music work that Stability and ElevenLabs are explicitly targeting?**
5. Klay signed all three majors before it shipped anything; Suno shipped first, got sued, and is worth $5.4 billion. **Which of those is the model the next founder should copy — and what does your answer say about whether copyright law is actually functioning as a deterrent?**

## Open questions

- **Stable Audio 2.5's exact launch date.** Stability's own news page renders a date that parses as 10 September 2024, while VentureBeat, AI Business and MBW all covered it as a September 2025 release "17 months after Stable Audio 2.0" (April 2024). Settled by: the Wayback Machine's first capture of the Stability news page, or Stability's changelog.
- **Has Suno's licensed Warner model actually shipped?** The November 2025 deal said new licensed models would arrive in 2026 and current models would be deprecated. As of 2 August 2026 there is no announcement that this has happened. Settled by: suno.com/release-notes or a WMG press release.
- **Has Udio's UMG platform launched at all?** The changelog suggests no. Settled by: Udio or UMG announcement, or restoration of downloads.
- **Klay Vision's actual launch and product.** Signed all three majors in November 2025, "mid-2026" launch reported by secondary sources only. Settled by: klay.vision product availability or an NMPA/label confirmation.
- **Whether OpenAI's music model exists.** Everything traces to The Information's October 2025 reporting; OpenAI has never confirmed. Settled by: an OpenAI announcement.
- **The real "AI-touched" share of new music.** Deezer only detects 100%-AI recordings from known models. Nobody publishes a number for partially AI-assisted tracks, and DDEX disclosure is self-reported. Settled by: an independent audit or a DSP publishing disclosure-tag statistics.
- **What Suno, Udio and ElevenLabs actually pay rightsholders per generation.** Every deal's financial terms are undisclosed. The NMPA template's 50/50 composition/recording split is the only public number. Settled by: a rightsholder publishing royalty statements, or discovery in the Boston case.
- **Chinese market figures.** Mureka's ~$12m annualised revenue and MiniMax per-generation pricing come from aggregator coverage, not filings. Kunlun Tech is listed, so its interim reports would settle the revenue question.
