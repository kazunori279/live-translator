# Discussion prompts — the moderator's ammunition

**TL;DR for the assistant:** Everything here is a question you can say out loud, and every one of them is answerable from the eight research files in this folder — the supporting file is named in brackets after each. Use **opening provocations** to start, **by theme** to go deeper, **escalation ladders** when the panel agrees too fast, **devil's advocate lines** when the room gets warm and boring, **scenarios** when the argument has gone abstract, and the **Tokyo** set when you want the audience in it. Facts, dates and links to back any of these up are in [`00-index.md`](00-index.md).

---

## Opening provocations (6)

Six questions that split a room within thirty seconds. Pick one, not two.

1. **More than half of all new music uploaded to Deezer is now fully machine-generated — about 90,000 tracks a day — and it takes one to three percent of listening. Is that a crisis, or is that the market working exactly as it should?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md, 01-generative-music-landscape.md)*

2. **Ninety-seven percent of nine thousand listeners could not pick the AI track in a blind test, and the stated preference for human music collapses within sixty seconds of actually listening. If the difference isn't audible, what exactly are we protecting?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

3. **Warner and Universal sued the companies they said stole from their artists, then settled, licensed and took upside — and are now being sued by their own session musicians for not passing the money through. Did the majors win this for artists, or sell them?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md, 04-artists-practice-backlash.md)*

4. **Two days ago a Munich court held that Suno's models contain reproductions of six copyrighted songs. Nine months ago a London court held that Stable Diffusion's weights "at no point contained copies" of anything. One of those courts is wrong about how the technology works. Which one?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md, 05-technical-foundations.md)*

5. **A track generated end-to-end from a one-line prompt has no US copyright — the Copyright Office says prompts alone don't make you an author, so anyone can copy it freely. Is that the right answer, and would you apply it to your own work?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

6. **Suno's CEO said "the majority of people don't enjoy the majority of the time they spend making music." Nick Cave says songs "arise out of suffering." Both of them are describing the same activity. Who is describing it accurately?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

## By theme (6 themes, 4–5 questions each)

### Theme A — Money: who actually loses?

A1. **CISAC projects AI takes about 60% of music library revenue by 2028 but only about 20% of streaming revenue. Is "AI is killing music" honest when the exposure is overwhelmingly in the anonymous, work-for-hire end the industry always treated as disposable?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md, 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

A2. **Spotify paid out eleven billion dollars in 2025 and its hundred-thousandth-ranked artist earned $7,300. Does AI change that number in either direction, or is it a distraction from a distribution problem that predates it?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

A3. **Pro-rata payouts mean uploading 90,000 tracks a day is a rational strategy even if nobody listens. Would switching every service to user-centric royalties solve the AI flood — and why hasn't anyone with the power to do it done it?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

A4. **The first US AI streaming-fraud conviction ended in an $8,091,843.64 forfeiture for a bot farm running 661,440 streams a day. Is AI music primarily an artistic phenomenon or a financial-crime one — and which department should be handling it?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

A5. **Four days ago IFPI rolled out global chart-eligibility principles: a record charts only if it is "substantially human made," made with an authorised AI service and labelled to consumers. Eleven companies wrote them — the three majors, HYBE and the big indies — and the first charts to enforce them are MENA, Southeast Asia, South Africa and Latin America. Is that the industry finally drawing a defensible line, or eleven companies deciding who is allowed to be popular in markets that had no seat at the table?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

### Theme B — Law: does any of this deter anyone?

B1. **Suno shipped first, got sued, and is worth $5.4 billion. Klay signed all three majors before shipping anything and nobody can name a Klay song. Which one should the next founder copy, and what does your answer say about whether copyright is functioning as a deterrent?** *(see 01-generative-music-landscape.md)*

B2. **Anthropic paid $1.5 billion — about $3,000 a work — for pirated books, after a judge called the training itself "exceedingly transformative." Is that a price signal or a rounding error?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

B3. **The Munich court reached Suno's US training by taking jurisdiction through a collecting-society forum and then applying US fair use law itself. Should a German court be deciding American fair use, and what happens to the internet if every country does that?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

B4. **The EU AI Act's enforcement powers went live today with fines up to €15m or 3% of turnover, and Article 50 requires AI content to be marked. Does a transparency regime with real fines change anything if the watermarks it depends on don't survive a re-encode?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md, 05-technical-foundations.md)*

B5. **A hacker leaked Suno's own source code three weeks ago and it names the sources: two million clips from YouTube Music, plus Pond5, Genius and Deezer. Suno's answer is that it always said it trained on "publicly available" music. Anthropic was told its training was "exceedingly transformative" and still paid $1.5 billion — for how it got the files. Does knowing exactly how the audio was acquired change the copyright question, or only the price?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

### Theme C — Voice, likeness and consent

C1. **Grimes offered a 50/50 master split to anyone using her AI voice, three years ago. Almost nobody followed her. Was that a failed experiment, or the only sane licensing model anyone has proposed?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md, 04-artists-practice-backlash.md)*

C2. **Tennessee's ELVIS Act lets you sue the maker of a tool whose primary purpose is cloning voices. The federal NO FAKES Act would preempt state laws like it. Is one national standard worth trading away the strongest protection artists currently have?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

C3. **ByteDance's Seed-Music does zero-shot singing-voice conversion from ten seconds of audio. Every artist alive has published more than ten seconds. Is consent a coherent concept here, or are we legislating for a world that already ended?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md, 05-technical-foundations.md)*

C4. **Denmark proposed a likeness right lasting fifty years after death. Should your voice outlive you — and if so, who gets to answer the phone?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

### Theme D — Craft: what are musicians actually doing?

D1. **Seventy-one percent of musicians use AI for stem separation and under a quarter use it to generate songs. Is that a stable equilibrium, or just where the tools happen to be good today?** *(see 04-artists-practice-backlash.md, 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md)*

D2. **Eighty-two percent of producers say they're against generating a full song from a text prompt, while eighty-seven percent already use AI somewhere in their workflow. Where exactly is the line they're drawing, and can any of you state it as a rule?** *(see 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md, 04-artists-practice-backlash.md)*

D3. **Magenta RealTime 2 hits about 200 milliseconds of control latency and runs on a laptop. At what latency does a generative model stop being a machine you operate and start being a musician you play with?** *(see 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md)*

D4. **Holly Herndon credits her model Spawn as "an ensemble member." Is that a meaningful artistic claim, a rhetorical move, or a way of avoiding the credit question?** *(see 04-artists-practice-backlash.md, 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

D5. **The Beatles' "Now and Then" won Best Rock Performance in 2025 with a lead vocal machine-learning had extracted from a 1977 cassette, and the Grammy rulebook lets an AI-voiced track win in a songwriting category so long as the human authorship is "meaningful and more than de minimis." Generate the hook, write the verses yourself — does that pass? Give me your own de minimis line.** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md, 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md)*

### Theme E — Meaning, disclosure and the label

E1. **Eighty percent of listeners want AI music labelled and sixty-nine percent want it paid less, but ninety-seven percent can't hear it. Is a label a consumer-protection measure or a purity test?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

E2. **The July 2026 industry labelling standard distinguishes "AI-Generated" from "AI-Assisted" and covers sound recordings only — not lyrics, composition, video or cover art. Once "AI-assisted" includes Auto-Tune, stem separation and a Synthesizer V vocal, does the label carry any information at all?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md, 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

E3. **The Velvet Sundown had a million monthly listeners and lost the room the day it was exposed, not the day it was released. What was actually taken from those listeners?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

E4. **Ted Gioia says AI art "will inevitably feel empty and hollow" because the machine has no self. If the hollowness can't be detected by ear, is that a claim about the music or a claim about the listener's beliefs?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

E5. **You can derive that generating a six-minute track costs about 0.3 watt-hours — one chatbot question — but you have to derive it, because not one music-model developer publishes an energy figure and music generation isn't even a task in the public benchmark. Is the environmental objection to AI music answered by the arithmetic, or is the arithmetic itself the evidence that the disclosure regime we're all demanding stops at the studio door?** *(see 05-technical-foundations.md)*

### Theme F — History: is this just the drum machine again?

F1. **Twenty-two thousand theatre musicians lost their jobs to sound film after 1927, and the union president privately admitted that fighting progress would destroy the union. Is 2026 the talkies, or is it different — and name the difference.** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

F2. **The Linn LM-1 was blamed for ending session drumming — and its sounds were sampled from working session drummers. Is "the displaced supplied the weapon" a bug of this technology or its defining feature?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

F3. **Gregory Coleman played the Amen break, it built two genres, he died homeless, and the only money anyone ever paid was an £18,000 fan crowdfunder in 2015. Did the industry earn the right to be outraged about uncompensated training data?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

F4. **Auto-Tune went from Time's fifty worst inventions to a standard instrument in fifteen years. Name the AI tool that makes that same journey, and the one that never will.** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md, 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md)*

## Escalation ladders (5)

Each ladder is three rungs: a mild opener, a harder follow-up if the panel agrees too easily, and a third that forces a specific, on-the-record commitment.

### Ladder 1 — Consent and training data
1. **Should AI companies need permission to train on recorded music?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*
2. *If everyone says yes:* **Then Japan's Article 30-4 permits exactly that without permission, and JASRAC's own surveys find only about half of Japanese creators object. Is the other half wrong, or is "consent" doing less work than you're assuming?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md)*
3. *Commitment:* **Give me the price. What is a fair per-track, one-time licence fee for including one recording in a training set — a number, not a principle. And would you sell your own catalogue at it?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md, 01-generative-music-landscape.md)*

### Ladder 2 — Labelling
1. **Should AI-generated music be labelled?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
2. *If everyone says yes:* **China has legally mandated labelling since 1 September 2025, with embedded metadata on all AI output and platform-level verification. Do you want that system, including the enforcement apparatus that makes it work?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md)*
3. *Commitment:* **Watermarks don't survive a re-encode — nine schemes were tested against twenty-two attacks and none held. So who pays for detection, what's the penalty for a false positive on a human artist, and would you accept your own next release being mislabelled?** *(see 05-technical-foundations.md)*

### Ladder 3 — The settlements
1. **Were the Warner and Universal settlements good for artists?** *(see 01-generative-music-landscape.md)*
2. *If everyone says yes:* **Then why is the American Federation of Musicians suing Universal and Warner rather than Suno and Udio, alleging the settlements licensed members' recordings with no "new use" payment and no credit?** *(see 04-artists-practice-backlash.md, 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*
3. *Commitment:* **Suno is fighting to keep the Warner settlement sealed. Should the terms be public — and if you're on a label, will you ask yours to publish what it got and how it's splitting it?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

### Ladder 4 — Is AI music actually a threat?
1. **Does AI-generated music threaten working musicians?** *(see 04-artists-practice-backlash.md)*
2. *If everyone says yes:* **AI tracks are over half of new uploads and one to three percent of listening, with eighty-five percent of those streams fraudulent. The measurable damage is bots, not audiences. Are we defending musicians or defending an anxiety?** *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*
3. *Commitment:* **CISAC says the loss lands on library and production music. Should the industry spend its political capital protecting the jingle-and-bed-music sector — yes or no — and if no, say out loud that those jobs are acceptable losses.** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

### Ladder 5 — Authorship
1. **Can a machine be a co-author?** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
2. *If everyone says no:* **Then Thaler settled that in US law and the Copyright Office says prompts alone don't make you an author either — so a fully generated track belongs to nobody and anyone may copy it. Is that outcome acceptable to you?** *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*
3. *Commitment:* **Draw the line. How much human input makes it yours — lyrics only? A re-sung vocal? One edited bar? Give a test a registrar could actually apply, and note that JASRAC and NexTone already apply one: demonstrable human creative contribution.** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md, 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

## Devil's advocate lines (12)

One-sentence provocations for when the panel has agreed with itself for too long. Deliver flat, then stop talking.

1. **"If AI music is so bad, why did ninety-seven percent of listeners fail to spot it?"** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
2. **"Nobody in this room objected when the Amen break made two genres and paid its drummer nothing."** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
3. **"The people most confident that AI can't make real music are the people who never had to make a library cue by Friday."** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md, 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*
4. **"Spotify was already commissioning authorless background music from humans in 2017 — AI just lowered the price of something you were all fine with."** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
5. **"Your objection isn't to the machine, it's to the terms — you'd license it tomorrow at the right rate, and Warner already did."** *(see 01-generative-music-landscape.md)*
6. **"Only five point seven percent of the training hours are non-Western, so this whole panel is arguing about who owns a machine that can only play Western music anyway."** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
7. **"HYBE burned thirty-five million dollars on AI voice and liquidated the company — maybe the market already answered the question we're debating."** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md)*
8. **"Auto-Tune was one of Time's fifty worst inventions and it's on the record you released last year."** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*
9. **"Half a million people have paid to watch a synthetic singer in this country, so let's stop pretending the audience is the one with the objection."** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md, 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md)*
10. **"Everyone here wants disclosure and nobody here wants their own stem separation, pitch correction or session-player replacement disclosed."** *(see 04-artists-practice-backlash.md, 06-performance-tools-cocreation.md)*
11. **"Generating a song costs about as much electricity as one chatbot question, so on carbon grounds you should object to your own streaming habits long before you object to the machine."** *(see 05-technical-foundations.md)*
12. **"The Recording Academy already gave a Grammy to a record whose lead vocal was pulled off a 1977 cassette by a machine-learning model, so the line was crossed before any of you noticed."** *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

## Concrete scenarios (6)

Read the scenario, then ask: **who, if anyone, is wronged — and what is the remedy?** None of these has a clean answer; that is the point. Don't accept "it depends" without a follow-up.

**Scenario 1 — The consenting estate.**
A major label releases an album by a dead artist's cloned voice. The estate consented and is paid. There is no disclosure on the release, and the vocal is convincing enough that nobody asks. Who is wronged — the fans, the artist who can't object, the session singers who would have sung the demo, or nobody? *(see 02-law-copyright-litigation.md for digital-replica law and Denmark's 50-year post-mortem proposal; 07-culture-authorship-labor.md for disclosure and the Velvet Sundown reaction)*

**Scenario 2 — The Yoasobi model.**
A developer trains a model exhaustively on one living band's catalogue and ships it. The outputs share only style and worldview; no protected melody or lyric ever appears. Japan's 2024 guidance says this probably does not infringe — though a minority of the committee disagreed loudly enough to be written into the main text. The band's sync income halves within a year. Has anyone been wronged, and by what? *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md; 02-law-copyright-litigation.md for market dilution in Kadrey)*

**Scenario 3 — The honest ghost.**
A woman in a small town writes every lyric herself, generates all the music and the vocal with Suno, releases under an invented persona, charts on Billboard radio airplay and signs for three million dollars. She discloses the AI. A label CEO passes on her only because "that's not going to be a touring entity." Is she a songwriter, a producer, a fraud, or the first honest version of something the industry has done for decades? *(see 07-culture-authorship-labor.md; 03-industry-economics-platforms.md)*

**Scenario 4 — The session player's own recording.**
A session bassist played on a hit in 2014 under a standard work-for-hire agreement. In 2025 his label settles with an AI company and licenses the master for training. He is not paid, not credited, and not asked. The label says his contract already covered it; his union says this is a "new use." He can't strike, because as an independent contractor coordinated action risks antitrust liability. Who owes him what — and if the answer is "nobody, legally," is that a defect in the contract or in the law? *(see 04-artists-practice-backlash.md; 02-law-copyright-litigation.md; 07-culture-authorship-labor.md)*

**Scenario 5 — The mislabelled human.**
A streaming service's AI detector flags an independent artist's genuinely human bedroom recording as fully AI-generated. It is demonetised and excluded from editorial and algorithmic playlists. Detection is proprietary, there is no published accuracy rate, and the appeal takes six weeks. Is the platform wrong to run the filter, wrong to run it silently, or right on both counts because the alternative is 90,000 fake tracks a day? *(see 03-industry-economics-platforms.md; 05-technical-foundations.md)*

**Scenario 6 — The clean model that nobody can afford.**
A company trains only on music it commissioned and owns, discloses its full corpus, pays every contributor, and ships a model that is clearly worse than the one trained on everything. A competitor trained on scraped audio wins the market. Consumers can't tell the difference and don't check. Was the ethical company wrong to try, is the market wrong, or is this simply what "voluntary licensing" means in practice? *(see 01-generative-music-landscape.md; 05-technical-foundations.md; 08-japan-asia-scene.md for SOUNDRAW's in-house-only claim)*

## Questions for a Tokyo audience (5)

1. **Japan has the most permissive AI-training rule in the G7 — Article 30-4, which a Waseda professor called a "machine-learning paradise" back in 2017. JASRAC now wants it amended so creators can refuse. If Japan does that alone and nobody follows, have Japanese creators gained a right, or have Japanese AI companies just lost a market while foreign models keep training on J-pop anyway?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md)*

2. **Hatsune Miku turns twenty next year, Magical Mirai has drawn over 580,000 people, and Miku V6 shipped in April. But Crypton stripped licence terms from AI-generated works on its own platform in 2023. Does Vocaloid prove Japan is ready for synthetic artists — or that Japan already found the one model that works and generative AI is breaking it?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md)*

3. **China made AI labelling legally mandatory in September 2025 and Korea followed in January 2026 with fines and a compute threshold. Japan's AI Promotion Act has no penalties at all — which is why CODA had to write OpenAI a letter instead of filing a claim. In five years, which of those three laws will Japanese musicians wish their government had passed?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md)*

4. **The piapro Character License let anyone use Miku non-commercially without asking, and a twenty-year fan economy grew on top of it. That is a pre-cleared derivative-works licence — the exact thing generative-AI platforms are being sued for not having. Why has nobody copied it, and would any Japanese rightsholder in this room offer one for their catalogue?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md; 02-law-copyright-litigation.md)*

5. **HYBE spent about thirty-five million dollars on Supertone and resolved to liquidate it in July 2026 — weeks after Tencent Music told investors AI songs are a growing share of daily new releases, and while no Japanese major has announced a generative-music licensing deal at all. Is Japanese caution strategic patience, or is the market being ceded to Shenzhen and San Francisco while everyone waits for the Agency for Cultural Affairs?** *(see 08-japan-asia-scene.md; 01-generative-music-landscape.md)*
